Use 0–3 only after collecting evidence: 0 = blocked/contradicted; 1 = assertion only; 2 = specific example with partial support; 3 = direct evidence or demonstrated access. Record the source for each score. Fatal blockers override totals. If evidence is weak, keep the decision open.

### First discovery actions

1. Identify 5–8 relevant prospects across factory operations, maintenance/reliability, and procurement; prioritize people who experience or fund the problem.
2. Ask about recent real events before presenting GridMind. Record examples and exact consequences, current workarounds, data owners, budget path, objections, and next steps.
3. Request records only with explicit authorization; identify who can approve data access and who can independently review findings.
4. After understanding the problem, explain the bounded concept and current limitations. Do not promise savings or imply unverified production capability.
5. Ask for a concrete next step: introduction to the budget owner, follow-up on a named case, permitted sample data, or a bounded paid evaluation.
6. Review all three candidates against the same rubric. Compliments and general interest are not willingness-to-pay evidence.

### Core questions

- Tell me about the last time this problem happened. What changed and what was the consequence?
- How often has it occurred in the last 6–12 months? What records support that?
- Who detected it, who made the decision, and who can approve spending?
- What tools, spreadsheets, experts, or vendors are used now? Where does that process fail?
- What data exists, who owns it, and could a limited review be authorized?
- What result would change a decision, and who would judge its correctness?
- What could make the result misleading or unsafe?
- If a bounded assessment worked, what would the next decision and payment route be?
- What would prevent participation even if the problem is real?

For every conversation record date, role, candidate, recent example, consequence, workaround, data access, authority/budget path, objection, agreed next step, and confidence. Separate direct evidence from interpretation and avoid unnecessary personal details.
