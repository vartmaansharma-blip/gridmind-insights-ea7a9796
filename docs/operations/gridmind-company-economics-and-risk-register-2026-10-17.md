# GridMind — Company Economics, Operating Model & Risk Register
**Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Class:** Supporting financial/operational decision workbook in Markdown, NOT an alternative roadmap, price list, legal opinion or evidence of buyer demand.  
**Authority:** [Master Plan](../gridmind-master-plan-2026-10-11.md) controls. Numeric examples below are **explicitly fictional assumptions** used to expose failure cases; replace only with permissioned, verified pilot data in the canonical discovery ledger and company accounts.

## 1. Economic objective and choice architecture
The company is not optimized for number of modules, AI API calls, document count, website animations, gross bill size, or noncollectable receivables. It is optimized for **durable risk-adjusted operating cash flow**, honest industrial outcomes and the option to expand into profitable adjacent accounts.

**Opportunity attraction ≈ buyer willingness to pay × feasible repeat frequency × gross contribution, constrained by acquisition friction, specialist competence, legal rights, customer payback and data risk.** This expression is qualitative; don't treat it as an objective score.

One firm, one workflow core, **only one commercial entry lane** after customer evidence. Subsequent divisions require separate investment proposals. Reuse is a hypothesis to test, not automatically a margin improvement.

## 2. Economic buyer and value proposition
| Lane | Buyer | WTP evidence that matters | Delivery costing trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 quality + C1 | Owner, plant head, quality procurement champion | Previous external 8D/CAPA consulting spend, repeated rejection cost, explicit paid next step | Domain reviewer time, repeated shop-floor data chasing, responsibility for results |
| R2 acceptance/invoice | Finance/commercial owner | Payments delayed *due to remediable evidence gap*, previous dispute cost and defined commercial scope | Being mistaken for a lawyer, collections agency or lender; customer buyer not cooperative |
| R3 electricity exposure | Owner/plant-finance energy sponsor | Material unresolved bill/contract exposure after existing accountant/electrician review | Tariff/licensed-specialist analysis and too few repeats |
| Enterprise supplier programme | Tier-1 quality/procurement sponsor | Budgeted multi-party pilot with signed data rights | Enterprise onboarding and conflicts of interest exceed contract value |

**Never** count the entire rejected order/invoice value as recoverable GridMind value. Separate face value at risk, actual incremental cost, causal attribution, and the amount a buyer is willing to spend on a resolution.

## 3. Fully loaded per-case record (mandatory before paid scale)
| Category | Definition / formula | Recording control |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed price | Commercial offer and scope/version | Quote is not revenue |
| Contracted | Signed obligations, milestone date | Appropriate adult/legal oversight |
| Invoice amount | Issued and accepted | Invoice is not collection |
| Cash collected | Cash actually received and reconciled | Collection date and adjustments |
| Expert fee | Invoiced/contracted work by qualified reviewer | Competence and independence |
| Founder service labor | Hours × realistic shadow compensation | Don't set to zero for appearance |
| Employee/contractor labor | Direct case hours × rate | Distinguish fixed salary allocation |
| Travel/field work | Actual approved visit cost | Qualified adult/site supervision |
| Software/AI/storage | Allocated attributable cost, inc. reprocessing | No client content in unapproved providers |
| QA/rework | Time to correct deliverable | Include failed and refunded cases |
| Payment fees | If applicable and lawful | No financial custody in MVP |
| Direct cost | Sum of attributable variable delivery costs | Avoid double counting reviewer fees |
| Gross contribution | Cash collected − direct cost **for cash planning** | Separately calculate accrual gross profit |
| Acquisition + onboarding | Qualified lead effort, sales time, setup cost | Report outside direct gross margin |
| Contribution after CAC | Collected cash − delivery − acquisition/onboarding | CAC only once per customer; allocate renewal appropriately |
| Operating profit | Recognized revenue − all operating costs | Not the same as gross margin |
| Runway | Unrestricted cash ÷ monthly net cash outflow, if positive | No fictional cash balance |
| Working capital | Collection delay relative to time vendors/staff are paid | Review before expanding credit exposure |

**Avoid a blended “AI margin” model**: measure each lane separately; service delivery, support and customer procurement differ.

## 4. Illustrative sensitivity: monthly managed resolution package
**Nothing here is current GridMind pricing.** Suppose the contract price is ₹30,000/month, variable professional/support/delivery cost ₹12,000/factory-month and fixed overhead ₹1,80,000/month.

| Active factories | Revenue | Direct cost | Gross contribution | Operating result before tax/financing* |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | ₹1,50,000 | ₹60,000 | ₹90,000 | −₹90,000 |
| 10 | ₹3,00,000 | ₹1,20,000 | ₹1,80,000 | ₹0 |
| 20 | ₹6,00,000 | ₹2,40,000 | ₹3,60,000 | ₹1,80,000 |
| 30 | ₹9,00,000 | ₹3,60,000 | ₹5,40,000 | ₹3,60,000 |

*Assumes fixed overhead and variable cost per customer stay constant. At scale that is unlikely: extra reviewers, acquisition, assurance and on-site travel may raise costs. These numbers are not a revenue projection and do not reflect taxes or financing.

**Breakeven** with these assumptions = ₹1,80,000 / (₹30,000 − ₹12,000) = **10 active paying factory-months**. Not ten prospects; ten collected/current-paying engagements with the assumed cost structure.

**Adverse case:** ₹15,000 selling price and ₹18,000 variable cost → **−₹3,000 per customer before overhead**. Adding customers destroys cash and cannot reach profitability.

**Cash warning:** a ₹30,000 invoice unpaid for 90 days pays neither reviewers nor bills. Track accounts-receivable aging, refunds and working-capital demands.

## 5. Commercial experiment design and gates
**Research conversation:** a recent concrete event (not “would you like an AI tool?”), actual cost/time/decision, alternative and known residual gap, buyer authority and a non-confidential follow-up. Record exact permissions.

**Proposal experiment:** a one-question service scope, expert capacity and cost, price hypothesis, owner and delivery acceptance. Do not send an offer without adult/qualified professional approval.

**Paid pilot:** accepted terms and permissioned evidence, bounded delivery, reviewer evaluation, customer verdict, agreed outcome-verification method, collection record and negative feedback. Do not assert ROI without credible baseline and source-backed attribution.

**Repeatability:** measure mean/median and spread of specialist hours per comparable case, proportion of reusable steps, number of revisits, revenue per customer, support cost, acquisition payback and repeat purchase. Averages across three unrelated sectors are not meaningful.

**Scaling prerequisites:** multiple independent buyer groups, explicit demand at economically viable prices, known billing collection timings, operational capacity and rollback plan. Prefer signed repeat work to hypothetical TAM.

## 6. Suggested economic dashboard (internal, not public)
- Leads: legitimate source, permission, role, date, next step; no scraped contact dossiers.
- Pipeline: scoped potential engagements and actual actions.
- Delivered cases: case lane, authorized status, reviewer, version, accepted vs rejected findings.
- Financial: cash collected, uncollected invoices, direct cost, shadow labor, contribution, acquisition investment and fixed burn.
- Service quality: source conflicts, reviewer corrections, revoked reports, repeated customer complaints, actual verification rate.
- Repeat business: paid continuation, churn/no-renewal reason, next action and time to value.
- Service capacity: reviewer queue, expected work hours, available skilled personnel and stop rules.

Avoid public charts of “money saved” with generic engineering estimates. Investor metrics should use collected revenue and audited costs where available, with honest uncertainty.

## 7. Founder operating model / people plan
**Founder:** one decision log, customer interviews, evidence governance and product scope. A student founder does not enter industrial sites, sign complex customer agreements, or authorize electrical work independently.

**Supporting adult:** consented outreach routes, commercial/legal authority and site visit supervision; agreement to help is currently **unconfirmed** in canonical records.

**Domain reviewer:** external qualified quality engineer or authorized finance/tariff professional as chosen by lane; reviewer independently states scope, competence, conflicts, expected hours, fee and willingness to stop on evidence insufficiency.

**Technology:** Lovable presentation and Codex/back-end quality; retain current framework. Do not hire permanent product specialists before revenue case.

**Professional advisers:** legal/privacy/contracts, accounting/tax, cyber/data security, industrial safety and insurance appropriate to actual risk. Verify applicable rules with counsel; no unsourced regulatory promises.

**Hiring thresholds:** first investigate constrained part-time qualified reviewer; add second reviewer only if queue and economics warrant; full-time customer success only after recurring paid demand; enterprise sales team only after evidence of repeated large contracts.

## 8. Risk scale and response language
Likelihood P and Impact I are qualitative labels: Low/Medium/High/Unknown. **Unknown is not Low.** Stop-now severity means do not process customer data or claim technical approval until control is verified.

Risk owner roles are proposed, **not staffed**. A planned policy is not an implemented security control.

## 12. Incident and complaint playbooks (not yet exercised)
**Suspected data breach:** stop intake/publishing; preserve forensic evidence safely; revoke affected access; assess what data/rights are involved; contact competent security/legal incident responders and responsible adults; follow applicable notification and remediation duties; inform customers appropriately under advice; independently verify fixes before resume.

**Incorrect published technical finding:** suspend or mark withdrawn; trace source and reviewer revision; prevent unsafe reliance; notify approved recipients; obtain qualified re-review; issue correction with provenance and rollback/audit trail; do not erase old record.

**Payment dispute or bad delivery:** compare signed scope to delivered evidence; offer structured correction process; record refund/credit decisions with authorized contracting party; don't pressure buyers or hide defects.

**Unavailable reviewer:** stop review-dependent case progression, tell customer limitations and alternatives, do not replace qualified signoff with a model.

**Account/CI outage:** preserve branch and tests, identify platform quota vs actual compiler failure, avoid repeated speculative retries; only release from a verified, reviewed candidate SHA.

## 13. Monitoring cadence and board-quality evidence
**Weekly (during discovery):** actual routes, replies, held conversations and disconfirming cases, reviewer feasibility, financial burn/time. One canonical live tracker.

**At every case closure:** evidence completeness, revision-bound reviewer status, customer acceptance/decline, outcome quality, collected revenue and total direct service hours.

**Monthly once trading:** P&L and cash flow reconciliation, A/R aging, contribution distribution by case lane, repeat purchase, retention, incidents, control exceptions, founder capacity and risk appetite. Avoid vanity ARR if no binding recurring contracts.

**Before expansion:** new lane demand, customer overlap, domain competence, regulated/safety implications, multi-tenant release controls, delivery costs and explicit authorization. Reject expansion where fundamental economics are unproven.
