# GridMind Product Definition and Strategic Evolution

**Status:** Strategy and decision framework, not a claim of current product readiness. The [Decision Constitution](gridmind-decision-constitution-and-contradiction-register-2026-10-10.md) governs immediate scope and any conflicting strategic instruction.  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-11

## 1. The concise definition

**GridMind helps an industrial team make one consequential operating decision better by turning authorized, imperfect operational evidence into a traceable finding, a reviewed next action, and a measurable outcome.**

The company-level ambition is to expand this repeatable decision loop across industrial workflows. The product must earn that breadth case by case; the phrase “industrial intelligence platform” is the destination, not a sufficiently specific first product.

Its long-term ambition is to become a trusted intelligence and coordination layer for industrial value creation—not simply a dashboard, an isolated AI agent, an energy-monitoring tool, or a marketplace.

The definition intentionally includes both intelligence and realization. Identifying an opportunity creates little durable value if a factory cannot make a decision, mobilize the right resources, implement an intervention, and establish whether the outcome improved.

### First-principles foundation and boundaries

The fundamental problem is not that factories lack dashboards or AI. It is the testable possibility that, for some costly recurring decisions, relevant evidence is fragmented or difficult to reconcile, the responsible people cannot confidently choose or coordinate an intervention, and the resulting business outcome is not reliably verified. This gap must be demonstrated in a real workflow; it must never be assumed from the existence of multiple systems.

The causal chain to investigate is:

**Operational event → measurable consequence or risk → detection → evidence gathering and reconciliation → decision → approved intervention → measured outcome → retained learning.**

GridMind creates incremental value only when it improves one or more links in this chain beyond the customer's current tools, experts, and processes. Detection alone is not value realization. An estimate is not a verified saving, and temporal sequence alone does not prove causation.

**Operating principles**
- **Value before features:** build a capability only when it addresses a specific validated need or is necessary to test a high-risk hypothesis.
- **Evidence before claims:** distinguish observed facts, estimates, hypotheses, expert judgments, and verified outcomes.
- **Narrow entry point, expandable architecture:** design reusable foundations where justified, but implement only the bounded workflow selected by evidence.
- **Integrate before replacing:** use existing systems where they already perform well; prefer simpler process changes when they achieve equal or better outcomes.
- **Human authority and safe boundaries:** consequential actions remain with authorized customer personnel and qualified experts.
- **Earn expansion:** add a new workflow only when a real customer need and a credible shared-core advantage justify it.

**Current unknowns:** the underserved gap, best buyer/segment, first workflow, willingness to pay, measurable incremental value, delivery economics, and durable differentiation. No customer validation, savings, or repeatable commercial outcome is implied by this definition.

## 2. The strategic distinction: destination versus entry point

These are different decisions and must not be conflated.

- **Destination:** a trusted industrial intelligence and coordination layer spanning relevant operational and economic workflows.
- **Initial entry point:** one narrow, measurable, urgent factory problem that GridMind can address with available authorized evidence and a repeatable delivery method.
- **Expansion mechanism:** reuse the same factory context, evidence lineage, prioritization, intervention and verification workflow to solve adjacent problems.
- **Defensibility hypothesis:** accumulated permissioned industrial context, high-quality evidence lineage, validated diagnostic methods, intervention/outcome history, repeatable integrations and trusted distribution. These are hypotheses to earn—not existing moats to claim.

GridMind should not select energy as its permanent identity simply because energy is the current product emphasis. Energy and utility losses are candidates for the first wedge, to be compared with other costly and measurable problems during customer discovery.

## 3. How to choose the first wedge

Score candidate problems against the following dimensions before committing:

| Dimension | Decision question |
|---|---|
| Economic pain | Is the problem frequent, material and owned by a clear budget holder? |
| Data access | Can the customer provide legitimate, traceable and sufficiently complete evidence? |
| Verifiability | Can we establish a baseline and measure a post-intervention result? |
| Urgency and willingness to pay | Is the problem important enough to fund now, rather than merely interesting? |
| Time to value | Can GridMind produce a useful, reviewed result quickly enough? |
| Repeatability | Can the method transfer to another facility without extensive custom engineering? |
| Actionability | Is there a realistic intervention, accountable owner and path to implementation? |
| Risk | Can data, safety, cyber-security and operational risks be controlled appropriately? |
| Commercial access | Can we reach the plant, data owner, engineering lead and economic buyer? |

A weighted score can support comparison, but it must not substitute for customer interviews, actual data access, willingness-to-pay evidence or observed outcomes. Do not select a winner from desk research alone.

Possible candidate wedges include energy/utility losses, avoidable demand costs, compressed-air system inefficiency, asset reliability, unplanned downtime, process waste and other facility-specific losses. These are hypotheses to test, not claims that GridMind already detects them accurately.

## 4. The core product loop

**CONTEXTUALIZE → QUALIFY EVIDENCE → DETECT → INVESTIGATE → PRIORITIZE → DECIDE → COORDINATE → VERIFY → LEARN**

1. **Contextualize:** understand the facility, process, asset, operating schedule, production state, relevant constraints and economic context.
2. **Qualify evidence:** check provenance, units, timestamps, completeness, quality, authorization and comparability; explicitly report exclusions and uncertainty.
3. **Detect:** identify a defensible deviation or opportunity without claiming unsupported causes.
4. **Investigate:** distinguish observations from hypotheses and present the evidence needed for a qualified person to validate possible causes.
5. **Prioritize:** compare opportunities using potential economic value, confidence, cost, risk, disruption, time to benefit and verifiability.
6. **Decide:** support the authorized customer and qualified experts in selecting whether and how to act.
7. **Coordinate:** connect the decision to owners, work orders, procurement, equipment, service providers or other required resources where useful.
8. **Verify:** compare appropriate post-intervention evidence with a defensible baseline and account for material operating changes.
9. **Learn:** record what was confirmed, rejected, implemented and verified, under explicit data rights and governance.

Not every use case will require every step or every integration. The loop defines the shared product logic, not a mandate to build a large suite before the first outcome is proven.

## 5. The economic decision layer

GridMind should not rank opportunities by estimated savings alone. A useful decision record should include, where relevant:

- observed problem and evidence references;
- confidence and known limitations;
- estimated gross opportunity and recoverable portion, with assumptions;
- intervention cost and ongoing costs;
- downtime, production disruption and safety considerations;
- implementation owner and dependencies;
- time to benefit;
- verification method and required post-period data;
- status: candidate, reviewed, approved, rejected, implemented or verified.

Do not collapse confidence, expected value and feasibility into a single unexplained score. Keep the underlying dimensions visible so customers can understand why an opportunity is prioritized.

Financial estimates are not realized savings. Verified outcomes require an appropriate measurement and review method, including treatment of changes in production, operating hours, weather, tariffs or other relevant factors when applicable.

## 6. Expansion path over ten years

The years below are conditional strategic horizons, not forecasts, delivery promises or a reason to build ahead of evidence.

### Stage 1 — Prove a narrow economic outcome (roughly years 1–2)

- Choose one problem and a clear facility/customer profile.
- Obtain customer-authorized real data and establish a reproducible evidence contract.
- Deliver a useful finding with qualified domain review.
- Track the resulting decision and intervention.
- Measure the post-intervention outcome without presenting estimates as verified savings.
- Establish willingness to pay and delivery economics.

**Gate to progress:** at least one credible real-factory end-to-end result, followed by a repeatable result at a second facility; independent review, data/security checks and commercial evidence must support the claim.

### Stage 2 — Become useful in adjacent workflows (roughly years 2–3)

Add a second operational problem only when it shares enough of the existing data/context model and a customer has a real need. Candidates could include related utilities, asset performance, maintenance, reliability, production loss or quality-related waste.

**Gate to progress:** shared components materially reduce delivery cost; the product remains understandable and auditable; customers pay for recurring value rather than one-off bespoke analysis.

### Stage 3 — Connect decisions to purchasing and implementation (roughly years 3–5)

Integrate procurement intelligence where it follows naturally from a validated opportunity. Examples include repair-versus-replace comparisons, lifecycle cost, quote normalization, supplier qualification, service-provider discovery and intervention coordination.

Supplier recommendations must be based on transparent, relevant criteria. Commercial relationships must not secretly bias recommendations. Customer data and quote information must be permissioned and protected.

**Gate to progress:** evidence that these workflows improve customer decisions or implementation outcomes and produce sustainable unit economics.

### Stage 4 — Build a permissioned industrial network (roughly years 5–7)

Support multi-facility customers and connect factories with qualified OEMs, distributors, maintenance firms and specialists. Use partner-led delivery where it is safer and more capital-efficient than building a large field-services organization.

Develop cross-site comparisons only where data definitions and operating contexts are sufficiently comparable and customer permissions permit the use. Protect commercially sensitive information and clearly communicate benchmark limitations.

**Gate to progress:** repeatable deployment, trusted partner quality, meaningful retention and defensible network utility—not simply the number of integrations or listings.

### Stage 5 — Expand into industrial intelligence and coordination (roughly years 7–10)

If prior stages validate the opportunity, GridMind could become a cross-facility layer for operational intelligence, investment prioritization, sourcing and intervention coordination, and verified outcome management. It may extend to additional industrial segments—including data-center infrastructure—only when domain access, technical capability and commercial evidence justify it.

The aim is to become deeply embedded in how customers identify, decide, execute and learn from industrial improvements. The company should not attempt to own every physical service or supplier category; it should coordinate the value chain where its evidence, trust and workflow position create an advantage.

## 7. How GridMind can overlap with adjacent business models

- **Procurement intelligence:** add quote analysis, supplier-performance evidence and sourcing only when linked to an identified operational need. Do not launch a general procurement marketplace first.
- **Vertical AI operations:** use industry-specific workflows, structured records, approvals and narrowly scoped agents inside the product. Avoid generic agent features without measurable workflow value.
- **Industrial service/sourcing network:** start with customer-requested referrals and transparent qualification; expand only when demand and partner quality are demonstrated.
- **Data-center power intelligence:** treat it as a possible future vertical, not an automatic expansion. It has distinct buyers, infrastructure, risk and technical requirements.
- **AI security/governance:** build the access controls, auditability, approvals and provenance GridMind itself requires. A separate security product is not necessary unless independent customer demand emerges.

The rule is: **expand around the same economic outcome and reusable evidence-to-verification system—not by accumulating unrelated businesses.**

## 8. Potential sources of defensibility

These advantages are not present merely because they appear in the strategy. Each must be developed and validated.

1. **Industrial context graph:** relationships among facilities, processes, assets, measurements, constraints, interventions, suppliers and outcomes.
2. **Evidence lineage:** reproducible reasoning from source data through exclusions, calculations, assumptions and conclusions.
3. **Validated methods:** performance measured against expert-reviewed real cases, including false positives, missed opportunities and reasons analysis was refused.
4. **Intervention and outcome history:** what actions worked, failed or could not be verified in specific contexts.
5. **Deployment learning:** lower integration and expert effort per facility while maintaining correctness.
6. **Workflow and distribution:** trusted integration into customer decisions and a reliable network of qualified implementation partners.
7. **Permissioned network learning:** aggregate or benchmark only when rights, privacy, confidentiality and comparability permit it.

Do not claim a proprietary data moat before obtaining meaningful, legally usable data and demonstrating that it improves results.

## 9. Business model hypotheses

Potential models to test in sequence:

1. **Paid economic discovery/diagnostic:** a bounded engagement that produces an evidence-backed opportunity assessment.
2. **Recurring facility/site subscription:** ongoing monitoring, analysis, workflow and verification where the use case warrants recurring use.
3. **Implementation or partner coordination revenue:** transparent fees for real coordination value, with conflict-of-interest controls.
4. **Outcome-linked fees:** only where attribution, baseline, counterfactual, measurement method and contractual terms are agreed in advance and independently defensible.

These are hypotheses, not a final pricing decision. Test willingness to pay, gross margin, delivery effort, sales cycle and retention with real customers. Avoid pricing that rewards exaggerated estimates or discourages honest uncertainty.

## 10. Non-negotiable guardrails

- Synthetic/demo data must be visibly labelled and never represented as real factory evidence.
- Missing data is not zero.
- An observation is not a diagnosis.
- A hypothesis is not a fact.
- An estimate is not realized or verified savings.
- No monetary claim without valid inputs, explicit assumptions, currency and traceable calculations.
- Fail closed when evidence is insufficient, invalid, stale, incomparable or unauthorized.
- Distinguish data/instrumentation faults from possible physical faults.
- Initial OT integrations remain read-only unless a separately reviewed, explicitly authorized control design is established.
- Consequential recommendations require qualified human review.
- Do not bypass tenant isolation, access controls, RLS or authorized customer processes.
- Do not share customer data or use it for cross-customer learning without appropriate rights.
- Do not allow supplier commissions or undisclosed commercial incentives to dictate recommendations.
- Do not call the product pilot-ready until the current end-to-end path has been demonstrated and independently reviewed on real evidence.

## 11. Immediate priority

The immediate priority is proof, not feature expansion:

1. Refresh read-only system and access readiness.
2. Establish legitimate customer/admin-authorized access to one real factory and a specific asset/problem.
3. Obtain traceable, quality-qualified data and the operating/economic context required for analysis.
4. Run the current canonical analysis path and preserve source IDs, assumptions and exclusions.
5. Have a qualified independent domain reviewer assess the finding and its limitations.
6. If an intervention is justified and authorized, record it and define verification before action.
7. Measure the post-intervention outcome and report only what the evidence supports.
8. Repeat the process at a second facility and assess delivery economics.
9. Only then choose the next adjacent workflow using customer demand, shared data/context and repeatability.

The repository's system-alignment audit dated 2026-10-10 is the current constraint document: it says to pause feature expansion and not call GridMind pilot-ready until this end-to-end path is demonstrated on real evidence. Recorded live-readiness figures in that audit are a historical snapshot and must be refreshed before operational decisions.

## 12. The ten-year vision

**GridMind aims to become the trusted intelligence and coordination layer for industrial value creation: understanding how facilities operate, identifying where measurable value may be created, helping customers decide and mobilize the right resources, and learning from outcomes that have been verified under clear evidence and data-governance rules.**

The ambition is broad; the execution must remain narrow, evidence-led and commercially disciplined.


## 13. Conceptual contract: what GridMind must actually do

The earlier platform definition was directionally useful but too broad to guide a first product by itself. This section makes the operational contract explicit.

### 13.1 The customer job

Help a named person responsible for an industrial outcome answer one consequential question they cannot answer quickly or confidently with their current workflow:

- What changed, and what evidence supports that conclusion?
- Which issue or opportunity deserves attention first, and why?
- What should a qualified person check or decide next?
- Did the approved intervention improve the outcome, relative to a defensible baseline?

These are candidate jobs, not a promise that every current build can answer them. The selected first workflow must focus on one question and one decision owner.

### 13.2 The unit of value

The unit of value is **not a dashboard, alert, generated report, AI answer, or detected anomaly**. It is a decision-ready, evidence-traceable case that helps a responsible team take an appropriate action and determine whether the intended outcome occurred.

A case is only decision-ready when it identifies:
1. The decision owner and operational context.
2. The question being investigated and why it matters.
3. The authorized source records, time window, units, and data limitations.
4. Observations separately from hypotheses and causal claims.
5. Economic consequence with formula, assumptions, and uncertainty—or an explicit statement that it cannot yet be quantified.
6. Alternative explanations and checks that could disprove the leading interpretation.
7. The human reviewer, accountable action owner, and approval boundary.
8. The baseline and verification method defined before intervention.
9. The post-action result, including no improvement or inconclusive results.

A case that cannot meet the evidence requirements must say what is missing, not fill the gap with confident prose.

### 13.3 Minimum product loop

**Question → permissioned evidence → quality checks → bounded analysis → human review → approved action → outcome verification → reusable learning.**

- **Question:** a specific operational or economic decision, not an open-ended request to “find insights.”
- **Permissioned evidence:** only records the customer is authorized to provide and GridMind is authorized to process.
- **Quality checks:** validate identity, time, units, completeness, freshness, operating context, comparability, and source lineage.
- **Bounded analysis:** use transparent calculations and constrained analytical methods; label model-generated explanations as hypotheses unless independently supported.
- **Human review:** a qualified person checks material conclusions and determines whether action is appropriate.
- **Approved action:** GridMind does not autonomously control industrial equipment or bypass the customer's safety/change-control process.
- **Outcome verification:** compare against a pre-agreed baseline and account for relevant confounders; do not attribute all observed change to GridMind.
- **Reusable learning:** retain only data and derived information that the customer contract, permissions, security controls, and applicable law allow.

### 13.4 Product boundaries

GridMind is not initially:
- A universal factory brain that claims to understand every process.
- A generic chatbot placed over factory files.
- A replacement for ERP, MES, CMMS, QMS, SCADA, historians, or qualified engineers.
- An autonomous industrial-control system.
- A guarantee of root cause, savings, safety, or improved production.
- A marketplace whose recommendations can be distorted by undisclosed supplier incentives.

GridMind may integrate with existing systems when a validated workflow requires it. It should recommend a simpler existing tool or process improvement when that is sufficient.

### 13.5 First-product definition template

No candidate is selected until this can be completed with evidence:

- **Initial user:** [named role]
- **Economic buyer:** [named role / budget authority]
- **Triggering event:** [specific recent event]
- **Decision to improve:** [one decision]
- **Current method:** [tools, people, elapsed time, existing spend]
- **Failure in current method:** [observed gap, not assumed]
- **Required inputs:** [specific permitted records and context]
- **First useful output:** [one bounded deliverable]
- **Human approval:** [qualified reviewer and action boundary]
- **Outcome metric:** [measurable metric, baseline, time window]
- **Value calculation:** [formula, assumptions, uncertainty]
- **Time to first value:** [observed or explicitly estimated]
- **Delivery effort:** [hours and dependencies]
- **Willingness-to-pay evidence:** [actual behavior or commitment]
- **Reason to choose GridMind over alternatives:** [demonstrated incremental advantage]

If the buyer, trigger, input data, output, or outcome metric remains vague, the concept is not ready to build.

### 13.6 Architecture principles that follow from the concept

1. **Evidence before inference:** every material output must link to source facts and transformations.
2. **Deterministic economics:** financial calculations use tested, versioned formulas; language models may explain but must not invent numeric inputs.
3. **Tenant and permission boundaries first:** every query, record, artifact, and analysis run is scoped to the authorized organization/factory and actor.
4. **Explicit uncertainty:** missing or contradictory evidence reduces confidence or blocks a conclusion.
5. **Reproducibility:** preserve input snapshot/version, calculation/algorithm version, assumptions, exclusions, and output.
6. **Human control:** recommendations are advisory; the customer owns operational decisions and approved interventions.
7. **Measurement before claims:** estimates, opportunities, approved actions, and realized outcomes are separate states.
8. **Smallest useful workflow:** do not generalize a component until at least two validated cases show what genuinely repeats.
9. **Failure is a valid output:** “insufficient evidence,” “existing method is adequate,” and “no measurable improvement” must be representable outcomes.
10. **Commercial discipline:** every product capability must connect to a user job, buyer value, acceptance test, and maintenance cost.

### 13.7 What this changes now

This conceptual contract does not choose energy, downtime, procurement, quality, or any other wedge. It sharpens the criteria by which they will be compared. During validation, do not add broad features or redesign the marketing site to compensate for missing buyer evidence. The next decision is which narrow, buyer-owned job passes discovery, data-feasibility, delivery, and measurement gates.

**Status:** conceptual contract approved for planning; not evidence of implemented behavior, buyer demand, product readiness, or measured customer outcomes.


## 14. Refinement 2 — define the product at decision level, not platform level

This refinement resolves a remaining ambiguity: “help make a better decision” is still too broad unless GridMind defines the decision, the economic owner, the alternatives, and the evidence required to change action.

### 14.1 The real problem is a decision gap

GridMind should investigate situations where all four conditions coexist:

1. **Consequential:** the decision affects a meaningful operational or financial outcome.
2. **Unresolved:** the current process leaves a material question unanswered, answered too slowly, or answered with insufficient confidence.
3. **Addressable:** a responsible team can change something in response.
4. **Verifiable:** there is a realistic way to measure whether the decision or intervention improved the result.

A loss alone is not automatically a GridMind opportunity. A loss that cannot be influenced, distinguished from normal variation, accessed through permitted data, or economically justified may be outside the initial product.

### 14.2 Five entities the product must keep distinct

The data model, UI, analytics and commercial language should not collapse these concepts:

- **Signal:** a pattern, event, deviation or reported issue that warrants attention. It is not yet a confirmed problem.
- **Finding:** a claim supported by identified evidence and explicit limitations.
- **Opportunity:** a plausible improvement whose addressable value and feasibility are estimated, not yet realized.
- **Decision/action:** a human-approved choice with an owner, timing, and recorded status.
- **Outcome:** a measured result against a predeclared baseline and time window, with attribution limits stated.

These are separate lifecycle states, not synonyms. An AI-generated alert cannot silently become a finding; a finding cannot silently become a root cause; an opportunity cannot silently become savings; a recommendation cannot silently become an approved action.

### 14.3 The customer should buy an outcome-oriented workflow, not “AI”

The first offer must be expressed in customer language:

> When [specific event] occurs, help [named role] decide [specific decision] using [permitted evidence], so they can improve [defined metric] within [time window], compared with [current workflow].

If the team cannot complete this sentence with evidence, the use case is not defined well enough to build or sell.

The sales conversation should start with a recent incident and current workflow—not with an AI pitch, a list of features, or a generic promise to “optimize the factory.”

### 14.4 Value equation and honest economics

Use a customer-grounded equation appropriate to the use case:

**Potential gross value = baseline loss or cost × addressable share × plausible improvement rate.**

This is only an estimate and all factors must be independently supported or visibly marked as assumptions. Then estimate:

**Potential net value = potential gross value − implementation cost − operating/change cost − GridMind fees.**

Do not present either equation as realized savings. For any proposed case, report:
- Measurement unit and period.
- Source of baseline and data coverage.
- Normalization needed for production volume, product mix, shift, weather, input prices, maintenance state or other material factors.
- Addressable share and why it is controllable.
- Assumptions, ranges and sensitivity to uncertain inputs.
- Cost and time required to act.
- Confidence and unresolved confounders.
- Verification plan and conditions that would count as failure.

If these cannot be estimated credibly, GridMind should report the missing information rather than invent a monetary value.

### 14.5 Initial offer design: manual-first, software-assisted

Until repeatability is demonstrated, the first commercial offer may be a tightly bounded, human-reviewed service supported by software. This is an experiment, not a concession that GridMind must become an agency.

The first offer should define:
- One workflow and one decision owner.
- A strict input checklist and permitted data boundary.
- A fixed analysis scope and explicit exclusions.
- A named qualified reviewer for domain-sensitive conclusions.
- A tangible deliverable and acceptance criteria.
- A time-to-result target.
- A fixed or bounded delivery effort.
- A way to measure the customer's outcome.
- Clear handling of missing data, inconclusive results, and refund/fee terms to be agreed before any paid engagement.
- What can be reused across customers, and what must remain tenant-specific.

Automate only the repeated, high-friction parts observed in delivery. If each customer requires unique integrations, custom reasoning, or substantial expert time, record that as a threat to software economics.

### 14.6 Wedge selection: hard gates before weighted scores

Weighted scoring can create false precision. Apply fatal-blocker gates first:

**Gate 1 — Buyer:** a specific role experiences the problem and has authority or access to the economic buyer.

**Gate 2 — Event and urgency:** a recent, concrete example and the current response can be described.

**Gate 3 — Addressability:** a controllable intervention or decision exists.

**Gate 4 — Evidence:** required records and context exist and can be shared with permission.

**Gate 5 — Verification:** a credible baseline, outcome metric and measurement window can be agreed.

**Gate 6 — Differentiation:** current software, internal expertise, consultants and a simpler process have been considered; there is a plausible incremental advantage.

**Gate 7 — Delivery:** a small first result can be produced safely with available capabilities and bounded effort.

**Gate 8 — Commercial signal:** the buyer offers a concrete next step, such as introducing the budget owner, reviewing a scoped proposal, arranging authorized data access, or evaluating a paid test.

Only candidates that pass the relevant hard gates should be compared on frequency, economic impact, sales cycle, repeatability, gross margin potential, expansion and defensibility. A “no” or “unknown” at a gate must remain visible; it must not be hidden by a high market score.

### 14.7 The first product boundary

For the first validated use case, GridMind should do only the following:
1. Receive a defined set of authorized records and operating context.
2. Validate data identity, completeness, units, time alignment, freshness and consistency.
3. Produce traceable calculations and a bounded interpretation.
4. Expose uncertainty, alternative explanations and missing evidence.
5. Create a reviewable case with proposed next checks or options.
6. Record a qualified human decision and accountable owner, if the customer chooses to proceed.
7. Track outcome evidence against the agreed baseline.

Defer broad chat, generic agents, predictive maintenance, autonomous control, all-factory knowledge graphs, cross-customer benchmarking, supplier marketplaces and multi-vertical expansion unless a validated first workflow specifically requires them.

### 14.8 What would disprove the thesis?

We must be prepared to stop or change direction if:
- The current workflow already answers the decision well enough.
- The problem is infrequent, low-cost, or has no accountable owner.
- Better evidence does not change the decision.
- The necessary records cannot be accessed or aligned at acceptable cost.
- The result depends on a domain expert whose effort makes delivery uneconomic.
- The intervention is outside the customer's control.
- No credible baseline or counterfactual can be established.
- Buyers value the output but will not fund it, or procurement friction outweighs value.
- Similar cases do not repeat across facilities or the supposed reusable core fails.
- A simpler non-AI process provides equal or better value.

### 14.9 Current conceptual decision

**Decision:** Define GridMind as a decision-to-verified-outcome workflow with explicit lifecycle states, not a generalized insight engine. Keep the broader industrial intelligence platform as the long-term architecture hypothesis.

**Still undecided:** initial industry segment, buyer role, first workflow, pricing, implementation model, and whether the best entry point is operational loss, procurement, or another narrow workflow.

**Evidence status:** this is a product-design decision based on first principles. It is not customer validation, a production-readiness claim, or proof of willingness to pay.
