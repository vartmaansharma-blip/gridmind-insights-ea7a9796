## 20-minute interview guide

### Opening (1–2 minutes)
“I’m trying to understand the current workflow, including what already works well. I’m not pitching a finished product. Could we discuss the last time [broad workflow] became difficult?”

### Incident reconstruction (8–10 minutes)
1. What was the last specific occurrence? When and in what operating context?
2. What first indicated that something was wrong or needed a decision?
3. What happened next, step by step?
4. Which people/roles were involved, and who had authority to decide or act?
5. Which systems, records, spreadsheets, suppliers, OEMs or specialists were used?
6. What did the current process handle well?
7. What was delayed, missing, contradictory or repeated?
8. How was the issue resolved, and how was the outcome checked?

### Economic and alternative evidence (4–5 minutes)
9. What consequence occurred? Which inputs are measured, and which are estimates?
10. How often did comparable cases occur recently?
11. What current tool, vendor or process has already addressed the issue?
12. What is still unresolved, and what evidence supports that?
13. Who owns the metric, budget and purchase approval?

### Data, trust and next step (3–4 minutes)
14. Which records would be needed for a credible retrospective review, and who controls them?
15. What privacy, security, safety, legal or contractual approval would be needed?
16. What would count as a useful result—and what would count as failure?
17. Would it be appropriate to speak with another role, review a non-confidential workflow description, or define a bounded next test?

Do not ask for sensitive details or files during the first conversation. Do not record without explicit permission. If the person cannot discuss a specific incident, record that limitation and do not turn general opinion into validation.

## Evidence grading

- **A — Directly verified:** authorized source record or direct observation with traceable provenance.
- **B — Reconstructable:** source inputs and calculation identified; independent check pending.
- **C — Expert estimate:** informed estimate not reconciled to records.
- **D — Anecdotal:** general statement, hearsay, compliment or hypothetical.

For every important claim, capture the counterargument and what would disprove it. Separate facts, estimates, interpretations and hypotheses.

## 30-day target and review cadence

Target: 10 substantive conversations in 30 days, subject to legitimate access and oversight. This is a learning target, not statistical market proof.

- After every conversation: enter the record and update the strongest evidence/counter-evidence.
- Weekly: compare candidate workflows, buyer access, economic consequence, incumbent gap and permissioned data feasibility.
- Day 14: select the cheapest test that could disprove the leading candidate.
- Day 21: draft a retrospective protocol only if a real buyer and lawful data route exist.
- Day 30: decide select / narrow / pivot / partner / pause; explain the decision from evidence, including negative evidence.

## Success is not the number of messages sent

The sprint advances only when it improves understanding of a real workflow and produces a concrete next step. No reply is not demand validation. A positive comment is not willingness to pay. A sample file is not permission unless the right data owner authorizes it. A plausible calculation is not realized savings. A single interview is not product-market fit.
