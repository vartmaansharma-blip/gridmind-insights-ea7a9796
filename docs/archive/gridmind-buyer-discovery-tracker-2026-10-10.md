## Objective

Select one narrow GridMind offer only when actual buyer evidence supports a costly recurring problem, an authorized data path, a reviewable result, feasible delivery, and credible measurement. Do not infer demand from compliments, general enthusiasm about AI, website engagement, or our own technical preferences.

## Interview protocol

For every conversation, record:
- Date and participant role (avoid unnecessary personal data).
- Industry, plant type and relevant process context.
- Exact problem described in the buyer's words.
- Frequency and severity, including current workaround and who owns the budget.
- Evidence available and who can authorize its use.
- How the buyer currently investigates/solves the problem and time/cost involved.
- What result would change a decision.
- Required trust, security, integration and review conditions.
- Whether the buyer offered a specific follow-up, data pathway, paid test, or introduction.
- Disconfirming evidence and unresolved questions.
- Next action, owner and due date.

Ask about past behavior and real incidents before describing GridMind. Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use AI for this?” or treating hypothetical interest as willingness to pay.

## First discovery batch

Run an initial batch of **9 qualified conversations**: aim for three relevant buyer roles/organizations per candidate, adjusting for access and role overlap. This is a learning target, not statistically representative market research.

For each candidate, seek:
1. At least three problem-focused conversations with relevant buyers or operators.
2. One documented real past incident or workflow example.
3. A plausible authorized data path and baseline.
4. A named review role and economic decision-maker.
5. Explicit disconfirming evidence.

After nine conversations, review whether the sample is informative. If not, expand or narrow the segment rather than forcing a winner.

## Gate to select one wedge

Select a candidate only when:
- Multiple independent buyers describe substantially the same problem from actual experience.
- At least one buyer can identify a credible next step and authorize access to relevant evidence, subject to proper agreements.
- Inputs and output can be bounded and reviewed by a qualified person.
- A baseline and measurable success criterion can be agreed in advance.
- Delivery cost, risk and repeatability appear plausible.
- The offer can be described without unsupported savings, accuracy or superiority claims.

If none qualifies, do not select a winner. Revise the hypotheses or stop the weakest candidate.
