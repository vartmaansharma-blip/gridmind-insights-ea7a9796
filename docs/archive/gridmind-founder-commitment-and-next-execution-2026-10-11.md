## Commitment

GridMind remains the leading long-term industrial-AI thesis for now. This is a commitment to disciplined validation, not blind loyalty to a product hypothesis.

The ambition is to build a trusted industrial intelligence and value-realization company: help factories identify costly operational losses, establish what the evidence supports, coordinate appropriate human-approved action, and verify outcomes. The first paid use case is not yet selected.

## What we know—and do not know

### Known
- The industrial operations problem space is economically important enough to investigate.
- A narrow software or service-led experiment can test value before a broad platform is built.
- Buyer access, data access, trust, measurable incremental value, and repeatable delivery are major constraints.
- A narrow B2B AI service that can evolve into software is a credible alternative worth testing.

### Not yet established
- That a specific factory buyer has a sufficiently urgent problem for GridMind.
- That current tools and workflows fail in a way GridMind can materially improve.
- That relevant data can be accessed lawfully and safely.
- That GridMind can produce measurable, attributable improvement.
- That a buyer will pay, pilot, or grant a concrete next step.
- That GridMind is superior to the alternative business opportunities available to the founder.

No customer interviews, customer data, paid pilots, or savings are claimed in this document.

## The next target: a 30-day validation sprint

**Primary objective:** collect enough real-world evidence to decide whether to continue, narrow, or change GridMind's first commercial wedge.

### Targets
1. Seek 10 substantive conversations with manufacturing decision-makers or operators who have direct visibility into recurring operational losses.
2. Record the most recent real incident/workflow described, its consequences, current response, existing tools, and why the current approach is or is not sufficient.
3. Identify the budget owner and what evidence they would require to evaluate a solution.
4. Establish whether the required data exists, who controls it, and whether permissioned access is realistic.
5. Compare GridMind against a narrow B2B AI service-to-software opportunity using the same evidence discipline.
6. Do not expand product scope during discovery unless a concrete finding justifies a small, testable prototype.

These are targets, not completed activities or guaranteed outcomes. If access is unavailable, record that as a distribution constraint instead of inventing validation.

## Evidence standard

Separate:
- **Observation:** what the interviewee actually said or what was directly verified.
- **Interpretation:** what we think it means.
- **Hypothesis:** what remains unproven.
- **Decision:** what we will do because of the evidence.
- **Confidence:** why the evidence is strong or weak.

Do not count compliments, hypothetical interest, or “AI sounds useful” as willingness to pay. Prefer past behaviour, existing spend, repeat frequency, consequences, decision authority, and a concrete voluntarily offered next step.

Do not request confidential factory records. Obtain explicit permission before receiving any non-public operational data. Involve a trusted adult in formal commitments, contracts, payments, or sensitive industrial access; never visit a factory without explicit permission, suitable supervision, and site safety approval.

## Decision gates at day 30

### Continue GridMind
Continue if several independent conversations establish a repeated and economically meaningful problem, current alternatives leave a material gap, permissioned data is feasible, and a credible way to measure incremental value exists.

### Narrow or change the wedge
Do this if the problem is real but the proposed GridMind workflow is not the best way to solve it, or a different industrial workflow has stronger evidence.

### Test the B2B service-to-software alternative first
Prioritize it if a reachable buyer has a clearer problem, shorter path to a paid test, and a repeatable workflow that could become software.

### Reopen the consumer-brand alternative
Raise its priority only if a real distribution advantage, distinctive product insight, credible operations, and plausible repeat-purchase economics can be demonstrated.

### Stop or pause
Pause major build investment if buyer access remains absent, the problem is low urgency, data cannot be accessed, existing alternatives are sufficient, or measurable incremental value cannot be established. A pause is a rational decision, not a personal failure.
