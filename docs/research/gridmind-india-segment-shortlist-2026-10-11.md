# GridMind — India Segment Shortlist for Primary Discovery

**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** Desk-research shortlist only. No customer access, interviews, or demand are claimed.  
**Purpose:** Narrow the first buyer-discovery sprint without prematurely locking GridMind into a vertical.

## Recommendation

Start discovery with **small and mid-sized engineering / auto-component / metal-fabrication manufacturers** as the first segment hypothesis, while retaining **foundry / metal-processing** and **food-processing** as comparison segments.

This is not a final market selection. It is a practical starting shortlist because these are recognizable industrial operating environments where production, quality, maintenance, shift handover, and supplier records may exist. Whether the records are usable, the incidents costly, and the buyer willing to pay must be confirmed directly.

Do not target “all factories.” Pick one plant type, one buyer role, and one recurring incident class for each conversation.

## Why this shortlist

NITI Aayog's manufacturing roadmap groups multiple manufacturing sectors into clusters and emphasizes common value chains, customer segments, and ecosystem needs. It includes engineering (auto and auto components, construction/mining equipment, railway equipment, aerospace/defence), consumer products, electronics, life sciences, and chemicals. [NITI Aayog, Reimagining Manufacturing](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-10/Reimagining_Manufacturing_Indias_Roadmap_to_Global_Leadership_in_Advanced_Manufacturing.pdf)

NITI Aayog's material on India's MSME clusters notes the prevalence of clustered manufacturing and the importance of cluster associations and MSME development institutions as ecosystem stakeholders. [NITI Aayog — MSME cluster / green-transition roadmap](https://www.niti.gov.in/node/2102)

The Bureau of Energy Efficiency's SIDHIEE cluster portal lists industrial clusters and their industry composition, including mixed-industry and automobile-parts/metalworking units in Faridabad. [BEE SIDHIEE cluster details](https://sidhiee.beeindia.gov.in/ClusterDetails)

An IIM Ahmedabad study of automotive suppliers in India examines how exposure to OEMs and clusters relates to supplier performance and avoiding operational disruptions. This is a reason to examine supplier operating workflows, not proof of GridMind demand. [IIMA, 18 March 2025](https://www.iima.ac.in/publicationdoes-location-matter-study-automotive-clusters-india)

These sources establish that manufacturing clusters and engineering supplier ecosystems are meaningful places to investigate. They do not establish which segment is easiest for GridMind to enter or which has the highest willingness to pay.

## First interview target

**Target role:** plant/production manager or quality/maintenance lead at a small or medium engineering or auto-component manufacturer.

**Target event:** the most recent recurring production interruption, quality deviation, or rework problem that required people to gather information from multiple records or systems.

Why begin here: this makes the interview concrete and tests whether the evidence-reconciliation hypothesis is real. If the interviewee reports that their existing system already provides a fast, trusted investigation and the economic consequence is small, record that as disconfirming evidence and move on.

## Questions that discriminate between a real opportunity and a weak one

1. Tell me about the most recent incident that took longer than expected to understand.
2. What was the operational consequence, and which parts are actually measured?
3. How often does this happen? Can any record verify the frequency?
4. Which people and records were needed to understand it?
5. What was missing, inconsistent, or hard to find?
6. How long did the investigation take, and what decision was delayed?
7. What existing software, vendor, consultant, or internal expert already solves part of this?
8. Did the investigation change the action taken or prevent a repeat? What evidence supports that?
9. Who owns the issue and the budget?
10. What records could be used lawfully for a small test, and who would approve access?
11. What would make a result credible enough to use?
12. What specific next step would they be willing to take if the problem were important enough?

Avoid pitching AI, suggesting savings numbers, or asking the buyer to speculate about a product they have not seen.

## Decision logic

After the first three to five conversations (if access permits), compare:
- severity and recurrence of the problem;
- economic consequence with evidence;
- quality and permission status of records;
- inadequacy of current alternatives;
- access to the actual budget owner;
- willingness to take a concrete next step;
- plausible GridMind delivery cost and repeatability.

Three to five conversations are an early directional sample, not statistically representative validation. If one segment repeatedly yields only low-value administrative pain, do not keep it alive because it was the original favorite.

## Immediate next action

Find one legitimate route to a plant/production manager, quality manager, or maintenance lead in the engineering / auto-component supplier ecosystem. If no such route exists, document the access blocker and compare reachable contacts in the other two segments. Do not invent prospects, relationships, or interview results.
