## 5. Two-tier decision gates

### Tier 1 — Select a candidate for a bounded test

Use interviews and non-confidential evidence to assess:

1. **Specific pain:** a recent, concrete event described by a relevant role.
2. **Materiality and recurrence:** consequence and frequency, with measured facts separated from estimates.
3. **Residual gap:** why the best current tool, expert, or process did not adequately solve the problem.
4. **Ownership and access:** identifiable operational owner and credible route to the economic buyer; plausible source and authorized owner for any future evidence.
5. **Commercial signal:** explicit willingness to continue, introduce the budget owner, review a scoped proposal, or discuss a paid test—not merely “interesting.”
6. **Service feasibility:** a bounded first deliverable that can be produced honestly with available time and capability.

A candidate can advance to a **test-design stage** when evidence is promising and major blockers are understood. Baseline quality, causal incrementality, production security, and verified outcomes are not interview-only gates and must not be falsely marked passed.

### Tier 2 — Authorize a retrospective customer-data test

Before accepting or processing non-public data, require:

1. Named customer owner and data approver; written scope and data rights.
2. Data minimization, secure transfer/storage, retention/deletion plan, and approved recipients.
3. Qualified domain reviewer appropriate to the workflow.
4. Defined question, evidence provenance, alternatives, limitations, and human-review boundary.
5. Baseline and outcome metric with a feasible measurement window.
6. Incrementality/counterfactual plan appropriate to the claim; record confounders and uncertainty.
7. Safe delivery process, bounded founder/reviewer hours, direct cost, and liability limits.
8. Buyer-approved decision/use of findings and explicit success/failure/stop criteria.

If these gates cannot be met, stay offline with public, synthetic, or participant-approved non-confidential information, or stop. Do not imply a real customer validation.

### 15.1 Company definition, fundamental problem, and destination

**Working company definition:** GridMind is an industrial intelligence and performance-improvement company intended to help manufacturers understand operations, investigate consequential problems using evidence, make better-informed decisions, coordinate customer-approved improvements, and measure outcomes.

**Fundamental problem hypothesis:** Some industrial businesses may struggle to turn fragmented, imperfect operational information into a reliable explanation and a useful next decision about a costly problem. The gap may be evidence fragmentation, slow investigation, uncertainty, cross-team coordination, lack of actionability, or weak outcome measurement. It must not be assumed that every factory problem is an information problem; the cause may already be known while action is constrained by people, parts, capital, capacity, process, or management decisions.

**Long-term destination:** an industrial intelligence and value-realization layer. This is an ambition, not a claim of current capability, market leadership, or a validated moat. The company should earn expansion from one demonstrated workflow and reusable methods, not build a universal platform before validating a customer problem.

**Operating loop:** authorized evidence → traceable finding → qualified human review → customer-approved decision/action → measured outcome → retained learning only where explicit rights and permissions allow.

### 15.3 Provisional offer card

**Working name:** GridMind Production Loss Investigation (name is provisional).

**Offer hypothesis:** GridMind would help a manufacturer examine one specific, economically significant production interruption or recurring production-loss pattern that the existing investigation process has not adequately explained or resolved. Using customer-authorized evidence, a defined question, and qualified human review, the work would reconstruct what is known, assess plausible explanations and contradictions, document uncertainty, and identify the next checks or actions justified by the evidence.

**Customer is buying:** a bounded, traceable investigation and a better-informed next decision—not an AI model, dashboard, guaranteed root cause, guaranteed savings, or an automatic machine diagnosis.

**Initial outcome hypothesis:** the work produces a useful, evidence-linked decision brief that helps the customer determine what to verify, investigate, change, or leave unresolved. A finding of “insufficient evidence,” “existing process is adequate,” or “no defensible finding” can be a valid result.

**Three distinct value pathways:**
1. Investigation efficiency: less time spent collecting, reconciling, and summarizing information.
2. Decision quality: better-grounded choice among explanations or next tests.
3. Operational improvement: an approved intervention changes an outcome.
These must be measured separately. A better brief does not prove a better decision; a better decision does not prove realized savings; a post-intervention improvement does not by itself establish that GridMind caused it.

### 15.4 Case qualification before accepting work

A case should have:
1. A specific incident, repeated pattern, or operational question and a defined time window.
2. A credible description of operational/economic significance, separating measured facts from estimates.
3. A residual gap: what the existing staff, systems, consultant, maintenance/quality process, or investigation has not resolved.
4. A clear question and decision that the findings could inform.
5. At least one relevant, lawfully accessible evidence source and a person who can explain its context.
6. Customer authority to share/use the records for the stated purpose.
7. A bounded scope and feasible review path.
8. Appropriate expertise or an identified qualified reviewer.
9. A safe path that does not require unauthorized machine/control access or unqualified safety-critical work.
10. A plausible route to a buyer and a willingness to discuss the commercial terms of a defined service.

**Proposed screening heuristic only:** score seven dimensions 0–2 each—economic significance, investigative gap, evidence availability, bounded scope, customer participation, actionability, and delivery feasibility. A possible initial threshold is at least 10/14, with no zero for evidence availability, customer participation, or feasibility. This score is not validated, must not override fatal safety/data/expertise blockers, and should be revised after real cases. It is a case-screening aid, not a customer-demand score.

**Decline, defer, or narrow** cases with no defined decision, no credible evidence path, unauthorized data, unbounded scope, unsupported guarantees, unavailable necessary expertise, unsafe requests, or a problem that existing methods already resolve adequately. Do not accept work merely to obtain a pilot.

### 15.5 Proposed deliverable: Production Loss Investigation Brief

If a suitable case is authorized, the proposed deliverable is one concise brief with these components:

A. **Executive findings:** question, scope, why it matters, what is established, what remains unresolved, and the next decision.
B. **Event reconstruction:** a timeline of material events and records. Preserve the difference between when an event occurred and when it was entered into a system.
C. **Evidence register:** evidence ID, source/system/person, relevant period/timestamp, event/question addressed, source status, units/context where relevant, transformation, limitation, permission/handling status, and conflicting records.
D. **Findings and competing explanations:** each statement separated into observation, reconstruction, hypothesis, expert judgment, or verified result; show supporting and contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, missing data, and what would distinguish the hypotheses.
E. **Prioritized next steps:** a specific inspection, measurement, question, or approved action; rationale; proposed owner/role; required resources; safety/approval requirements; and a way to evaluate the result.
F. **Economic assessment, only where justified:** interruption duration, affected output, documented rework/scrap or other costs, estimated exposure, calculation method, assumptions, exclusions, and uncertainty.
G. **Follow-up/closure plan:** baseline, metric, observation window, operating conditions, confounders, outcome owner, and criteria for calling a result observed, implemented, or verified.

Economic terms must remain distinct:
- **Gross exposure:** estimated or recorded total cost associated with the event.
- **Potentially avoidable loss:** the portion plausibly addressable by an intervention, with assumptions and constraints.
- **Realized benefit:** observed change after action under a stated measurement method.
- **GridMind-attributable benefit:** incremental change credibly attributable to GridMind versus what would otherwise have happened, accounting for confounders and the limits of the design.
Do not present these as interchangeable.

### 15.7 Evidence, records, and minimum-data discipline

Potential sources, depending on the actual case, include production/shift logs, machine alarms, maintenance work orders, production schedules, operator observations, shift handovers, quality records, relevant process measurements, and existing incident reports. No source is automatically reliable merely because it is digital.

**Minimum viable evidence package:** (1) a defined problem, (2) a credible significance estimate, (3) timing or enough information to reconstruct the event, (4) at least one relevant operational source, (5) access to a knowledgeable person, (6) permission for the stated use, and (7) a specific decision the work may inform. If this package is not available, continue non-confidential discovery, request a suitable source, narrow the question, or stop.

For every material source:
- retain provenance and an immutable source reference where feasible;
- record period, units, time zone/clock assumptions, entry time versus event time, missing intervals, and transformations;
- normalize only when justified, document the transformation, and preserve the original values;
- investigate material contradictions; otherwise report them explicitly;
- do not infer missing values, asset mappings, causes, or costs silently;
- collect each item only when it addresses a defined unanswered question and its value justifies access and processing effort.

**Data governance:** obtain named customer authorization and purpose limitation before accepting non-public records; minimize collection; agree transfer/storage, access roles, recipients, retention/deletion, incident response, and any third-party use; separate customer workspaces and permissions; never commit real customer/factory records to GitHub; do not use records or derived data for public demos, model training, benchmarks, or cross-customer learning without explicit rights and appropriate safeguards. Never promise a technical privacy guarantee that has not been established by the actual architecture and workflow.

### 15.8 Delivery workflow and service boundaries

Proposed case workflow:
0. Non-confidential discovery and question definition.
1. Case qualification and explicit stop/go decision.
2. Written scope, roles, input list, handling terms, review needs, and completion criteria.
3. Authorized evidence intake and source register.
4. Timeline/event reconstruction and discrepancy log.
5. Investigation of competing explanations, with tests that could distinguish or falsify them.
6. Qualified technical review appropriate to the actual domain and risk.
7. Customer factual/context review.
8. Final brief, limitations, and next-decision discussion.
9. Agreed follow-up measurement if an intervention occurs.
10. Case retrospective: usefulness, errors, delivery time/cost, scope changes, repeatability, and commercial signal.

Customer review can correct context or source facts; customer disagreement alone does not prove a conclusion wrong or right. Material disagreement should be reconciled against evidence or recorded as unresolved.

**Turnaround hypothesis:** 5–10 working days after scope approval and receipt of the required evidence, excluding customer delays, reviewer availability, and agreed scope changes. This is an internal planning target only; it is not a public service-level promise until measured over real cases.

Written scoping should specify the problem/question, time period, permitted sources, customer owner, data approver, submission deadline, clarification rounds, reviewer role, deliverable, exclusions, change process, and what counts as completion.

### 15.9 Evidence and conclusion standards

Use these conclusion levels consistently:
1. **Observed:** a source directly records a measurement/event or a participant reports it (label the source type).
2. **Reconstructed:** multiple sources support a sequence or interpretation, with conflicts/limits noted.
3. **Hypothesized:** an explanation is plausible but alternatives remain; it is not a confirmed cause.
4. **Verified within stated limits:** an explanation or outcome has been tested adequately for the defined case and conditions, with method, scope, and limits recorded.

For each material hypothesis, record its statement, expected observations, supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, missing data, a discriminating test, and evidence that would weaken/falsify it. A model-generated answer cannot promote a hypothesis to verified status.

**Proposed quality standards for any bounded test:**
- 100% of material factual claims have a source or are explicitly labelled as reported, assumed, estimated, or uncertain.
- 100% of material calculations receive an independent check.
- Zero knowingly unsupported claims are presented as verified causes or outcomes.
- All material contradictions are resolved or disclosed.
- All customer material factual corrections are addressed and their disposition recorded.
- Every recommended next step includes rationale and an evaluation method.
- Agreed deliverables are completed, except documented and mutually agreed exclusions.
- Customer data is never used outside the authorized purpose.
These are process requirements/targets, not a guarantee of zero errors. Errors must be recorded, corrected, and considered for downstream impact.

### 15.11 Metrics and measurement plan

## 16. Economic Discovery Offer — Operating Specification v1 (provisional, 2026-10-13)

This section makes the proposed first engagement operational. It supplements, but does not replace, the canonical Master Plan, live discovery tracker, or Decision Constitution. It is not proof of demand and does not authorize customer-data processing.

### 16.1 Decision and positioning

**Offer format to test:** a bounded, human-reviewed Economic Discovery for one manufacturing site: investigate one agreed economic question using existing records and return an evidence-ranked decision brief.

**Lead use-case hypothesis:** energy and utility economics, because the customer-facing proof framework proposes this entry point and bills/tariff records may permit a lower-intrusion start than machine connectivity. This is a provisional offer variant, not a selected commercial wedge. C4 remains a discovery probe alongside C1. If discovery shows that the strongest unresolved problem is downtime/production loss, adapt the same bounded-engagement structure rather than forcing an energy analysis. The canonical status remains “no wedge selected” until real evidence passes Tier 1.

The customer buys a bounded investigation and decision-ready evidence package—not a dashboard, generic AI analysis, formal energy audit, engineering certification, guaranteed savings, or autonomous control.

### 16.2 Target customer and qualification

Before a customer-specific quote, require:
- A named operational sponsor and an identifiable economic buyer or credible route to one.
- A specific decision/economic question and site boundary.
- A customer able to authorize the proposed records and supply relevant operating context.
- A qualified domain reviewer who explicitly agrees to review the scope and conclusions.
- Agreement that the result may be “no defensible opportunity found.”
- A delivery scope that can be bounded safely and economically.

Positive fit indicators include meaningful utility spend; measurable output; usable historical bills and applicable tariff information; production and operating-calendar context where needed; a team able to investigate or act; and a genuine question not already adequately answered by the current process. These are hypotheses, not universal thresholds. Data-history requirements depend on seasonality, tariff, production mix, and the question.

Disqualify, defer, or narrow if there is no decision owner, data rights are unclear, evidence is inadequate, likely value is immaterial, a qualified reviewer is unavailable, or the customer expects guaranteed savings before analysis.

### 16.3 Unit of work and scope

The base unit is **one site + one agreed question + one historical window + one written decision brief**.

For the provisional energy/utility variant:
- Review agreed utility bills or equivalent records, applicable tariff/rate information, and production/output and operating-calendar context where available and authorized.
- Reconcile units, billing periods, site/meter coverage, tariff assumptions, missing periods, production denominators, and material anomalies.
- Identify only deviations/questions supported by the records.
- Estimate exposure or potential only when the formula, inputs, assumptions, exclusions, and uncertainty can be shown.
- Rank follow-up checks by evidence strength, plausible economic importance, actionability, and effort.
- Obtain qualified review before customer delivery.

Excluded: full plant energy audit; machine-level or root-cause diagnosis; power-quality assessment; electrical-panel inspection; equipment installation; live monitoring; real-time OT connectivity; tariff/legal advice; guaranteed savings; and safety-critical instructions. These require separate scope, competence, permissions, and measurement. No physical or electrical work is included.

### 16.4 Customer inputs and intake acceptance

Request only the information needed for the agreed question. A starting checklist is:
1. Written question, decision owner, site boundary, and period.
2. Utility bills or equivalent records, with dates and units legible.
3. Applicable tariff/rate schedule and demand-charge/contract-demand context where relevant.
4. Production/output totals and operating days/shifts if production-adjusted comparisons are proposed.
5. Known shutdowns, product-mix/order changes, equipment changes, holidays, tariff changes, and other context that may confound comparisons.
6. Existing analysis/audits/actions, if the customer chooses to share them.
7. A contact who can clarify records and correct factual misunderstandings.

Intake is not accepted until scope, period, source ownership, units, missing records, comparison basis, permitted use, retention/deletion date, recipients, and reviewer are recorded. Missing data means “unknown,” never silently zero. Do not request credentials, broad system exports, unrelated confidential information, or machine-control access.

### 16.5 Workflow and timing hypothesis

| Stage | Work | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| Qualification | Confirm decision, boundary, sponsor/buyer route, evidence, and current alternative. | Fit/no-fit recorded. |
| Scope and permissions | Agree question, records, period, roles, handling, exclusions, and stop conditions. | Written scope and permission. |
| Intake/data health | Check provenance, coverage, units, dates, duplicates, missingness, and comparability. | Gaps resolved, accepted, or scope narrowed. |
| Analysis | Run transparent calculations; test plausible explanations and alternatives. | Reproducible calculations and stated limits. |
| Domain review | Challenge technical plausibility, tariff interpretation, causal language, and actionability. | Review comments resolved or retained as uncertainty. |
| Customer review | Walk through findings and invite factual corrections/context. | Corrections and disagreements logged. |
| Closeout | Deliver brief and ledger; record usefulness, effort, costs, and next-step decision. | Delivery and outcome status recorded. |

**Timing hypothesis:** 5–10 working days after accepted intake for a suitably narrow case. This is not an SLA and must not be advertised as guaranteed until dry-runs and reviewer availability support it. Missing context, review delays, or scope changes pause the clock and require an agreed reset or re-quote.

### 16.6 Required deliverables

Every engagement produces:
1. **Decision brief (target 3–6 pages):** question, scope, conclusion, material findings, limits, and decision/next step supported.
2. **Opportunity/evidence ledger:** finding ID, source/period, observed facts, calculation, assumptions, data-quality flags, confidence rationale, counter-evidence, next discriminating check, owner role, and status.
3. **Reproducible calculation workbook or equivalent:** source references, units, formulas, exclusions, period alignment, and separation of source data from derived values.
4. **Data-health note:** coverage, missing/stale/inconsistent values, comparability problems, and conclusions that cannot be drawn.
5. **Ranked next-step list:** investigation/action, rationale, evidence needed, responsible role, likely effort/constraints, and success measure.
6. **Review/correction record:** reviewer role, material challenges, customer corrections, unresolved disagreement, and final disposition.
7. **Commercial closeout:** hours by role, direct costs, usefulness feedback, and whether a next paid step was proposed/accepted/declined.

A report is not successful merely because it contains many opportunities. “No material opportunity,” “existing process is adequate,” “insufficient evidence,” and “not economically justified” are valid outcomes.

### 16.7 Evidence and economic-calculation standard

Use explicit states: **Observed / Calculated / Estimated / Hypothesis / Recommended / Implemented / Realized / Verified / Sustained**. Never collapse them.

For every material economic estimate:
- State source, period, units, formula, and assumptions.
- Separate gross exposure from addressable opportunity and incremental value plausibly influenced by GridMind.
- Include intervention cost, implementation constraints, operational risk, and customer effort where known.
- Avoid double counting overlapping loss categories.
- Compare with the best realistic alternative—existing staff, process, software, or consultant—not “doing nothing” unless that is the true alternative.
- Use ranges or qualitative priorities when precision is unsupported.
- Do not infer cause from correlation or one unusual bill.
- Do not call an opportunity “savings” before action and appropriate measurement.
- Claim verified/sustained value only with a pre-agreed baseline/reference, observation window, adjustment for material confounders, and defensible measurement method.

An arithmetically correct estimate may still be commercially irrelevant. A potential opportunity matters only if it is plausible, addressable, actionable, and worth pursuing relative to effort and alternatives.

### 16.8 Roles, data governance, and safety

- **Founder/discovery lead:** coordinate scope, evidence ledger, calculations within competence, delivery tracking, and truthful labels.
- **Qualified energy/engineering reviewer:** validate technical/tariff interpretation and challenge claims. This role is not yet named or confirmed; no customer-specific technical energy analysis may be delivered until secured.
- **Customer sponsor/data approver:** authorize records, confirm context, and nominate decision owner.
- **Customer qualified personnel:** decide and perform interventions under their normal safety systems.
- **Appropriate adult/professional support:** review/support outreach and formal engagement.

Before receiving non-public data, require written scope/permission, data minimization, approved transfer/storage/access, named recipients, retention/deletion date, incident contact, and confirmation that the customer may share the records. Never put customer records in public repositories, issue trackers, public AI tools, or synthetic examples. Use a secure approved environment only after controls are checked. Customer data stays outside this documentation repository.

No live OT access, credentials, control writes, electrical work, panel inspection, or safety-critical instruction is included. Physical inspection must be separately authorized and performed by qualified personnel. Stop if authorization or safety is unclear.

### 16.9 Pricing method and willingness-to-pay test

**No fixed price is approved.** ₹15,000 remains an unvalidated discussion hypothesis—not a quote, market rate, or evidence of willingness to pay.

Calculate expected delivery cost using actual assumptions:
Delivery cost = founder hours × explicit internal rate + reviewer hours/fee + data preparation + travel/site cost + tools/processing + other direct costs.

Minimum viable fee = delivery cost + required contribution toward overhead, risk, and future product development.

Do not assume founder labour or specialist review is free. Do not invent a margin target without recording it as an assumption. Include acquisition/onboarding and likely rework. A price is not viable if it depends on unpaid specialist work or unbounded founder time.

Test willingness to pay in this order:
1. Learn what the customer currently spends—in money, staff time, audits, consultants, or delayed decisions—to address the same question.
2. Explain the bounded deliverables and exclusions without leading with speculative savings.
3. Identify the budget owner and approval process.
4. Quote only after reviewer availability, input feasibility, and delivery cost are understood.
5. Record the real response: accepted; counter-offer; procurement/approval needed; free-only request; interest without buyer; declined; or no decision.
6. Treat an approved purchase process or payment as stronger evidence than compliments or hypothetical answers.

Do not anchor all learning to ₹15,000. Do not offer a performance fee until attribution, baseline, measurement, contract terms, and risk allocation are workable.

### 16.10 Success, failure, and stop rules

Operational success requires:
- The agreed question is answered within evidence limits—or the evidence gap is diagnosed.
- Material claims are traceable and reviewed.
- The customer can state what decision, if any, changes.
- Factual corrections are confirmed or disagreements recorded.
- Actual delivery hours and direct costs are measured.
- A concrete next step is agreed, or no further work is documented as appropriate.

A useful report alone does not prove a repeatable business. Stop/narrow/decline if evidence cannot support a responsible conclusion; existing analysis is adequate; findings are not actionable; no reviewer is available; expected benefit cannot justify fee/effort/risk; certainty or guaranteed savings are demanded; work exceeds scope or authorized use; economics are implausible; or the workflow is so bespoke that repeatability is unlikely.

### 16.11 Closeout options

Offer only the next step supported by evidence:
- No further work, where no opportunity or residual gap is established.
- A customer-led check for a specific missing measurement or hypothesis.
- A bounded follow-up investigation with named owner, reviewer, scope, and fee.
- Measurement and verification only after an intervention, baseline, observation window, and measurement method are agreed.
- Recurring monitoring/subscription discussion only if a repeated decision need, maintainable inputs, action ownership, and economic usefulness are demonstrated.

Do not turn every discovery into a subscription pitch.

### 16.15 Synthetic dry-run — initial result (illustrative only)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 100,000 kWh | 10,000 units | ₹900,000 | 10.00 kWh/unit | ₹90/unit |
