# GridMind Attention Test — Creative Reset

**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** Design hypothesis for exploration; not a validated audience result.  
**Purpose:** Respond to the finding that the first three Reveal concepts are clear explorations but are not yet demonstrably remarkable.

## Honest assessment

The first three explorations are useful communication patterns, but none currently proves that GridMind would earn attention or be remembered. Context alignment, layered schematics, and evidence breakdowns are understandable, yet they are familiar enough that they could belong to many industrial analytics or consulting brands.

Do not label them as distinctive or attention-worthy until testing supports that claim.

## Strategic change

Stop asking only, "How do we show industrial complexity clearly?" Add a harder question:

**What is the one visual act that only GridMind would own—and that makes the visitor understand something at the same moment?**

The signature must be:
- identifiable in a still frame, not dependent on animation alone;
- describable in one sentence after brief exposure;
- repeatable across a homepage, product walkthrough, report, presentation, and future campaigns;
- connected to the product's real value, not a decorative brand gimmick;
- credible without fake telemetry, invented results, unsupported causality, or unearned product claims;
- legible on mobile and with reduced motion.

## Next concept hypothesis: The Cut

Explore one bold, consistent visual act: **a precise cut through an apparently complete industrial picture reveals that the original interpretation was missing context.**

This is a design hypothesis, not an approved identity. It must not become a generic split-screen before/after, a glowing scan line, or a cinematic effect without meaning.

Possible execution:
1. **First frame:** one visually arresting, tightly art-directed industrial composition with a single unresolved question.
2. **The cut:** a sharply bounded window or section moves through the composition; information on either side remains stable, while the revealed region introduces a meaningful contextual layer.
3. **Changed interpretation:** the viewer sees what was observed, what contextual information changed the question, and what remains unproven.
4. **Decision boundary:** end on a precise next question to investigate, not a fabricated answer or savings promise.

Test at least two art directions for this mechanism:
- **Editorial / typographic:** oversized type, a small number of exact annotations, a strong silhouette.
- **Spatial / industrial:** a custom, restrained abstract process composition with one cut revealing a second layer.

Do not copy Apple or Nothing. Use them as benchmarks for coherence and recognisability, not as templates.

## Required test

Show the first frame for five seconds, hide it, and ask viewers to describe what they remember. Then show the interactive sequence and ask:
- What do you think GridMind does?
- What changed when the reveal occurred?
- What do you remember that you would associate with this brand?
- What did you think the product was promising?
- What felt fabricated, confusing, or untrustworthy?

Include people unfamiliar with GridMind. Prefer relevant industrial buyers for commercial comprehension. Treat friends/internal reviewers as informal feedback, not buyer validation.

## Go / no-go

Do not proceed to a production homepage merely because the concept looks polished.

Advance only if the experience improves both recall and comprehension without increasing false beliefs about product capability. If it is memorable but unclear, simplify. If it is clear but forgettable, change the visual mechanism. If neither improves, return to the brand and product truth rather than adding effects.

## Scope protection

This work does not choose GridMind's initial commercial wedge, validate demand, approve claims, or alter src/routes/index.tsx. The existing Decision Constitution and product-readiness gates remain authoritative.
