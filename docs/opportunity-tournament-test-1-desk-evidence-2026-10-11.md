# Opportunity Tournament — Test 1: Evidence-Based Desk Test — 2026-10-11

**Test status:** Completed as a public-source desk test. This is not customer validation. No interviews, purchases, paid pilots, private datasets, or customer commitments are claimed.

## Question

Should the founder prioritize GridMind, a SuperYou-like creator-led consumer brand, or a narrow B2B AI service that could become software?

## Executive result

**The desk test does not justify choosing a winner. It does change what we should test next.**

- **GridMind:** The underlying industrial productivity problem is real enough to investigate. Public material describes Indian manufacturing AI and digitisation adoption, including textile MSMEs adopting AI and automation. But this does not prove that a specific factory buyer will pay GridMind, grant data access, or prefer it over existing methods. The first wedge remains unvalidated.
- **Creator-led consumer brand:** SuperYou is a real example of substantial reported growth, but its starting advantages are material: Ranveer Singh as co-founder, Nikunj Biyani's FMCG experience and network, and access to established brand-building capability. It is not a fair baseline for a founder without comparable distribution or operating capital. Press reports cite a revenue run rate close to ₹200 crore in February 2026; this is reported revenue/run-rate, not independently verified profit.
- **Narrow B2B AI service → software:** The strongest *first experiment* on a risk-adjusted basis if the founder can reach a business owner. It can be sold and delivered manually before building a platform. However, that is a structural hypothesis—not proof of demand for any particular workflow.

**Provisional order for the next validation sprint:** (1) B2B service-to-software, (2) GridMind's narrow industrial wedge, (3) creator-led consumer brand—unless the founder can demonstrate a credible creator/distribution advantage, in which case the consumer track deserves equal or higher priority.

## What the public evidence actually says

### 1. SuperYou: strong upside, founder advantages matter

Financial Express reported on 27 February 2026 that SuperYou, founded by entrepreneur Nikunj Biyani and actor Ranveer Singh, had a revenue run rate close to ₹200 crore and was targeting ₹1,000 crore turnover in five years. The article is a press report of company claims, not audited profitability data: https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/superyou-taps-better-for-you-market-in-growth-push/4157071/

A separate Financial Express profile describes Singh as a co-founder and discusses the pairing of his content/marketing strength with Biyani's FMCG experience: https://www.financialexpress.com/business/brandwagon-superyou-ranveer-singh-protein-wafers-fermented-protein-nikunj-biyani-3761101/lite/

Counterevidence matters: Financial Express reported in January 2026 that the broader D2C ecosystem had become more cautious about celebrity partners because visibility alone does not guarantee sustainable business performance: https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/direct-to-consumer-turns-wary-of-celebrity-partners/4119083/lite/

**Interpretation:** Do not copy the visible surface—protein wafers, celebrity branding, social buzz. Copy the underlying logic: a clear product insight, distinctive product, customer trust, distribution, and operational competence. Unless an actual creator partnership or efficient audience channel is available, assume the celebrity-distribution advantage is absent.

### 2. Industrial AI / GridMind: opportunity is plausible, buyer demand remains unproven

IBEF reported in June 2025 that textile MSMEs in Tiruppur were adopting AI and automation across parts of the manufacturing value chain; it reported an industry association's claim of a 10% production-efficiency improvement in that context: https://www.ibef.org/news/textile-industry-knits-business-with-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-companies-adopt-technology

IBEF's MSME digitisation overview describes the economic importance of MSMEs and ongoing adoption of digital tools: https://www.ibef.org/blogs/msme-digitisation-unlocking-productivity-and-market-access

**Interpretation:** This supports the relevance of industrial digitisation, not GridMind's product-market fit. Existing automation, ERP/MES products, internal spreadsheets, consultants, and operational experts are alternatives. GridMind must demonstrate a specific incremental improvement with permissioned data and a credible baseline. A large manufacturing market is not a substitute for buyer evidence.

### 3. General AI services / SME workflow automation: plausible route to earlier revenue

A recent industry research article reports that many Indian SMEs see potential in AI but face adoption barriers around cost and implementation support: https://www.mnbresearch.com/blog/insights-2/state-of-ai-automation-for-indian-smes-2026-356

Treat its specific survey percentages as directional rather than definitive because the article aggregates figures from different studies and does not establish an independently representative estimate in the page itself.

**Interpretation:** Implementation friction creates a possible service opportunity, but “businesses want AI” is too broad to sell. Pick one repetitive, expensive workflow with a budget owner; sell a defined result; then productize repeated delivery. Do not become a generic chatbot agency.

### 4. Consumer brands: distribution economics are the central test

Financial Express's recent analysis of consumer distribution argues that social platforms, marketplaces, and quick-commerce services increasingly mediate discovery and customer relationships: https://www.financialexpress.com/opinion/who-owns-the-consumer-now/4354286/

**Interpretation:** A consumer brand must prove that customer acquisition, contribution margin, repeat purchase, quality, fulfilment, and channel dependence work together. A waitlist, viral post, or influencer's reach alone is not proof of profitable repeat demand.

## Falsification questions for each path

### GridMind
1. Can we reach at least 3 relevant plant/operations/quality/maintenance decision-makers?
2. Do they independently describe the same costly recurring problem?
3. Can one share a recent example and describe the current workaround?
4. Is data available with permission and sufficient quality?
5. Can a bounded test measure improvement against a baseline?
6. Would the buyer pay for the result, and who owns the budget?

**Failure signal:** conversations stay abstract, no budget owner appears, no data can be shared, or current tools already solve the problem adequately.

### Consumer brand
1. What underserved customer and consumption occasion is being targeted?
2. What is the meaningful product advantage over alternatives?
3. Is there a real route to the first 100–1,000 customers without assuming celebrity-scale reach?
4. Can contribution remain positive after product, packaging, shipping, returns, channel fees and acquisition?
5. What is the evidence for repeat purchase?
6. What category-specific safety, labelling and quality obligations apply?

**Failure signal:** only compliments or social engagement, no willingness to pay, weak differentiation, or economics that work only under optimistic assumptions.

### B2B service → software
1. Can we reach 10 operators in one specific industry?
2. Is one workflow frequent, painful, and currently expensive?
3. Can we offer a human-reviewed, bounded service using approved data?
4. Will one buyer pay or commit budget to a pilot?
5. Can we measure time saved, error reduction, throughput, or another concrete result?
6. Does the same workflow recur across customers?

**Failure signal:** each customer requires a totally different solution, the pain is not budgeted, or delivery hours eliminate margin.

## Test 2: 7-day action plan

The next test must be real-world discovery. Public web research cannot validate willingness to pay.

### Day 1 — choose reachable buyers, not just attractive markets
Create a list of 10 reachable business operators for one narrow B2B workflow and 10 industrial contacts for GridMind. If the founder has a genuine creator/community/retail access route, identify 5 concrete partners or channels for the consumer track. Do not invent relationships or send messages without review/permission.

### Days 2–4 — conversations
Ask about the last actual incident or task, current workaround, frequency, business consequence, decision-maker, budget, and why existing options are insufficient. Do not pitch a broad platform before understanding the problem. Record direct evidence separately from interpretation.

### Days 5–6 — economics
For the two strongest problem candidates, estimate the value of the problem and cost to deliver a small outcome. For consumer products, use a hypothetical unit-economics model only until real supplier and channel quotes are obtained.

### Day 7 — gate
Score each opportunity on:
- urgency / frequency;
- budget owner and willingness to pay;
- access to customer and data;
- baseline and measurable outcome;
- repeatability;
- contribution margin;
- capital and downside;
- founder's genuine distribution/skill advantage.

Advance only a candidate with real evidence. If there is no access to real buyers, the test is inconclusive—not a pass.

## Decision rule

Do not decide “GridMind vs SuperYou” by comparing a software prototype with a successful company's reported revenue. Compare founder-specific starting conditions and next-step evidence.

**Provisional next move:** Run B2B service-to-software and GridMind discovery in parallel for one week. Keep a consumer-brand track only if a real distribution or product insight can be identified. After actual conversations, update the ranking with observed evidence and revise the GridMind strategy if the evidence points elsewhere.

## Source limitations

This is a public-source desk test, not a full market map. Company revenue figures are reported by press and may not disclose profitability. Industry adoption claims do not demonstrate demand for a particular product. No market-size number, survey statistic, or successful-company story establishes the founder's probability of success. Customer interviews, supplier quotes, paid pilots, and measured economics are the next evidence required.
