## 1. What we need

A qualified industrial energy professional to review the *method*, not certify GridMind, endorse a product, conduct an energy audit, or promise savings.

The first review should be limited to synthetic data and a short written review/call. The reviewer should have demonstrable experience with industrial electricity bills/tariffs, production context, energy-intensity calculations, and interpretation limits in manufacturing. Relevant BEE accreditation is a strong qualification signal; current status and relevant sector experience must be confirmed directly before describing anyone as an active accredited reviewer.

## 2. Review packet

Share only the public/synthetic document:
- [Synthetic Evaluation Fixture v1](gridmind-economic-discovery-synthetic-evaluation-2026-10-13.md)
- The offer/method section of the [GridMind Master Plan](gridmind-master-plan-2026-10-11.md)

The synthetic example has three invented monthly periods:
- January: 100,000 kWh, 10,000 units, ₹900,000 bill
- February: 110,000 kWh, 10,000 units, ₹990,000 bill
- March: 120,000 kWh, 9,000 units, ₹1,080,000 bill

It assumes a simplified ₹9/kWh variable rate and deliberately excludes fixed/demand/tax/other charges. The screening signal is that March's kWh per unit is about 33.3% higher than January. The naïve ₹270,000 comparison is explicitly *not* a validated savings claim because comparability, tariff components, production mix, operating hours, meter boundary, and other context are unknown.

The fixture also includes 12 synthetic edge cases: missing period, mixed units, tariff change, duplicate invoice, implausible consumption, changed operating context, unsupported cause, unsupported savings claim, missing denominator, conflicting invoice, baseline sensitivity, and a valid no-opportunity outcome.

## 3. Questions for the reviewer

Please challenge the method, including:
1. Are the arithmetic and unit conversions stated correctly?
2. What minimum bill components and billing-period details are required for a defensible invoice reconciliation?
3. What can and cannot be inferred from monthly kWh/output ratios without product-mix and operating-hour normalization?
4. What is the minimum evidence needed to compare baselines and describe a potential economic opportunity?
5. Which data-quality failures should block analysis rather than merely create a warning?
6. Which statements in the proposed brief could be misread as engineering conclusions, savings estimates, or formal audit findings?
7. What qualifications, site inspection, metering, or additional evidence would be required for a real plant-specific conclusion?
8. Is this bounded desk-analysis workflow useful as a screening step, or does it overlap with existing audit services without enough differentiated value?
9. What would a realistic review and delivery effort be? Please distinguish review time from full audit effort.
10. What changes are mandatory before any authorized real-data trial?

Ask the reviewer to classify each finding as **critical / major / minor**, and separate arithmetic corrections from scope limitations and commercial opinions.

## 4. What GridMind will not ask for

- No access to any factory, electrical panel, machine, operational technology, or customer system.
- No customer data, site drawings, credentials, or confidential records.
- No electrical/site work or safety-critical instruction.
- No certification, legal/tariff opinion, guaranteed savings, endorsement, or unpaid full energy audit.
- No use of the reviewer's name/logo or attribution without explicit written permission.
- No assumption that a short review substitutes for a full audit or an appropriately scoped engineering assessment.

If a fee is requested, ask for a written scope, deliverable, availability, and fee quote before agreeing. Do not promise payment or appoint anyone without an approved budget and appropriate adult/professional oversight.
