## 1. What a genuinely good GridMind MVP means

A good MVP is **the minimum complete business experience**, not the maximum software we can build with limited resources. A working dashboard is not enough; a promising interview is not enough; an automated calculation is not enough.

| Standard | Customer-facing proof | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Real urgency** | Recent, costly, unresolved decision described by a relevant buyer | Customer calls it interesting but takes no next step |
| **Clear scope** | One question, bounded site/period, defined exclusions and decision owner | “Optimize the factory” without a precise decision |
| **Better than status quo** | Demonstrably improves at least one important dimension of buyer's current approach (decision usefulness, effort, time, cost, traceability) | Same report the current engineer/consultant already provides without meaningful advantage |
| **Trust** | Every material finding traces to sources, limitations and competent human review | Confident AI diagnosis, unexplained figures or implied sign-off |
| **Actionability** | Buyer can choose a justified next step, including a request for additional evidence | Attractive charts without a decision |
| **Unit economics** | Actual fee, reviewer hours, founder hours, site effort, travel, rework and direct cost documented | A ₹15,000 price is assumed profitable without cost evidence |
| **Repeatability** | Similar cases use the same controlled method without a complete reinvention | Every case becomes bespoke consulting |

### Four different milestones—never confuse them

1. **Method prototype:** Completes a *synthetic* evidence-to-brief demonstration with reproducible results. No customer, diagnosis or revenue claim.
2. **Pilot-ready service:** One narrowly defined method, a qualified reviewer, enforceable permissions, appropriate security, safe data handling, and a feasible costed delivery procedure.
3. **First commercial MVP:** An actual buyer agrees to a paid scope and GridMind competently delivers a useful reviewed brief; payment/collection and costs are recorded accurately. A signed agreement and collected fee are distinct events.
4. **Repeatable business:** Further independent customers buy comparable work, reviewer quality holds, and delivery unit economics are demonstrated. One pilot **does not** prove product–market fit.

**Commercial design target, not a factual claim:** Make the first deliverable easy to understand, easy to buy, safe to try, and hard to dismiss as a generic AI-generated report.

### Strict definition of “ready” (five non-substitutable gates)

| Gate | Required observable evidence | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer/problem fit** | Named decision-making role, recent consequence, existing alternative, residual unresolved gap and a buyer-initiated concrete next step; satisfy *all* canonical Tier 1 criteria | Narrow the question, continue discovery, or stop. More code is not a remedy. |
| **Deliverable fit** | A buyer can explain what decision the brief supports, the scope and exclusions are acceptable, and a domain expert finds its method defensible | Redesign report or service boundary before adding automation. |
| **Technical trust** | Reproducible calculation or chronology, source provenance, fail-closed review, access tests and independent evidence-check examples pass | Block report release and customer-data intake. |
| **Delivery readiness** | Available appropriately qualified reviewer with defined paid scope/terms; permissions, secure custody, escalation and on-site roles agreed | No technical customer pilot. |
| **Commercial feasibility** | Actual case effort/cost plus buyer's price behavior and value relative to current alternative are documented; no negative-economic surprise is hidden | Reprice, narrow, redesign delivery or stop. |

**Do not average these gates into a readiness score.** An excellent UI cannot compensate for a failed safety gate or no buyer demand.

## 2. Define the product backwards from the customer deliverable

### 2.1 First thing to design: the Industrial Decision Brief

**Produce a realistic, fully marked SYNTHETIC sample of the actual document we want a buyer to purchase before building another dashboard page.** It must answer these questions in roughly one executive page plus evidence/technical appendices:

| Buyer question | Required report content |
|---|---|
| What was the decision? | Specific question, site/asset/billing boundary, period, and decision owner |
| What did you establish? | 2–5 prioritized **verified observations**, with evidence references |
| What remains unknown? | Missing, conflicting, unreliable evidence and explicit uncertainty |
| What could explain the issue? | Competing **hypotheses** and evidence for/against; no invented root cause |
| What should happen next? | Qualified-review-approved *investigation or decision options*, owners, cost/effort implications where supported |
| Is the money real? | Distinguish documented bill amounts, illustrative sensitivities, possible exposure, recoverable amount and *verified realized savings* |
| Who stands behind this work? | Reviewer identity/qualification/scope, decision, revisions, dissent, report version |
| How do we know it helped? | Outcome measure, baseline, review date and responsibility; record “not verified” when no measurement exists |

**Report release rule:** Draft analysis ≠ reviewed report. A report cannot be described as technically approved unless an appropriately qualified reviewer actually reviewed the identified scope and relevant sources. An inconclusive result is an acceptable—and often necessary—deliverable.

### 2.2 One tightly scoped offer—not a general factory platform

Use the exact buyer problem chosen through the canonical discovery gates. Current competing candidates:

- **C1 — Downtime / production loss:** “What does the documented event history establish about this recurring interruption, what remains unresolved, and which verification decision is justified?” Outputs: reconciled timeline, recurrence patterns, competing explanations, open checks. Does **not** promise autonomous fault diagnosis or direct equipment instructions.
- **C4 — Energy / electricity exposure:** “What do the authorized bills and relevant tariff/operating records establish about this cost concern?” Outputs: reconciled bill fields, period analysis, applicable documented billing checks, explicit unknowns. Does **not** automatically qualify as a statutory energy audit or prove avoidable savings.

**No wedge selected as of this revision.** Existing energy code or the C4 prototype is *sunk implementation context*, not a commercial tiebreaker. Use the same buyer/problem questions to evaluate both.

### 2.3 Make the offer purchasable

The eventual one-page statement of work (SOW) must specify: decision question; buyer/owner; boundary and dates; required permitted inputs; method and reviewer; excluded activities; delivery format; acceptance criteria; customer responsibilities; data rights/retention; treatment of inconclusive results; fee and payment terms; review/clarification limits; follow-up option.

**Pricing:** ₹15,000 remains a *hypothesis*, not our published or approved price. Quote only after estimating case-specific time, expert review, travel, tool use and contingency; avoid savings guarantees. Never imply factory engineers are being replaced.

### 2.4 Competitive replacement test — prove the switching reason

Our competitor is **the method the buyer would actually use tomorrow without GridMind**, not the weakest product on a comparison website. For each candidate and interested factory, complete this structured, interview-backed record before writing a value proposition:

| Field | Record separately for each real case |
|---|---|
| Decision moment | What exact decision was delayed, expensive or uncertain? What triggered attention now? |
| Existing process | Who normally handles it (engineer, electrician, accountant, auditor, CMMS/energy vendor)? What records/tools? |
| Best alternative's strengths | What does the current process handle well? Why might it already be sufficient? |
| Residual gap | What fact, verification, coordination or accountable decision is still missing, *after* their best current attempt? |
| Buyer switching trigger | What would make them authorize a test or pay instead of staying with that process? |
| Comparative burden | Existing total time, internal labor, cash spend, delay and dependencies versus proposed GridMind delivery, measured as possible |
| Credibility bar | Which person must trust/review the results, and what source or qualification will they insist on? |
| Disqualifiers | In-house team already solves the problem quickly; data unavailable; safety/data constraints; consultant is cheaper and better; no budget route |

**Competitive experiment:** show two fictional versions of the same decision—(A) what the existing process normally provides and (B) a GridMind-style synthetic evidence-linked brief. Make clear that A is a *hypothetical representation unless the buyer confirms it*. Ask for concrete improvements, omissions and which they would use, without presenting B as a real factory result. Then confirm through the buyer's actual process, not preference polling.

**Pass evidence:** the buyer describes a nontrivial residual gap, names a reason to switch, and takes an explicit next step toward a real bounded scope. **Failure:** they say their engineer/auditor already resolves it with equal or better quality at lower total effort. Record that disconfirmation.

### 2.5 Buyer acceptance test — will the Decision Brief earn its fee?

Show a **clearly marked synthetic** first page to the *decision owner* and, separately, a technically competent reviewer. Do not ask “Do you like it?” Use this five-question script and log verbatim objections:

1. “What precise decision would this let you make, defer or reject?”
2. “Which claim would you challenge, and which original record would you need to see?”
3. “How would your team answer this today, and where would this output change the result?”
4. “If the conclusion is inconclusive, what useful next measurement or decision would justify the fee—and when would it **not**?”
5. “Who owns the budget, and what specific next step would they take to review a scoped paid engagement?”

**Buyer-facing acceptance criteria in the future SOW:** scope and evidence sources disclosed, clear findings/limits, agreed minimum method applied, technical reviewer identified and signed within their competence, report delivered in agreed form, one clarification cycle specified, and confidentiality obligations honored. **Never guarantee a root cause, a savings amount or a positive conclusion.** If data gaps make a useful decision impossible, define a pre-agreed stop-and-communicate mechanism (scope adjustment, paused work or other agreed remedy)—do not conceal it in an expensive inconclusive PDF.

### 2.6 The sample report to create first (one executive page)

> **SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE — NOT A CUSTOMER RESULT OR TECHNICAL APPROVAL**\
> **Decision:** Should the plant commission additional checks regarding a documented change in monthly energy intensity?\
> **Established:** A source-linked synthetic comparison shows intensity increased between the illustrated periods.\
> **Not established:** The cause, comparability of operations, avoidability, recoverable charges or financial savings.\
> **Next decision:** Verify operating conditions, bill boundaries and applicable tariff details before choosing an intervention.\
> **Evidence:** E01–E03 (invented). **Expert review:** Not performed. **Customer-ready:** No.

Design the appendix backward from every statement on this page: evidence IDs, raw units, exact arithmetic, exclusions, alternative explanations, reviewer questions and version history. The finished sample must *visibly fail release* until real review gates are satisfied.

## 3. Structure the MVP as four connected operating systems

| System | Customer value | First version | Must *not* include yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Engagement & case scope** | Customer knows exactly what question will be answered | Written SOW, decision owner, investigation status, stop conditions | Sales CRM, payments platform, multi-site enterprise portal |
| **B. Evidence & analysis** | Records become traceable findings | Manual/permissioned intake, source register, verified fields, deterministic calculations or timeline, explicit limits | Unsupervised diagnosis, broad integrations, required new sensors |

### Stage 3 — Define pilot economics and delivery constraints (BEFORE EXPENSIVE ENGINEERING)

**Objective:** Check whether we can deliver professionally and sustainably.

Case-cost worksheet, per proposed engagement:

```text
Agreed fee (if any)                                      ₹ ______
Qualified reviewer: hours × agreed rate                  ₹ ______
Analyst/founder time: measured hours × shadow rate       ₹ ______
Site visits / travel / communications                    ₹ ______
Tools / document processing / other variable expense     ₹ ______
Rework / repeat review contingency                       ₹ ______
Direct delivery cost (carefully avoid double counting)   ₹ ______

```

Document where an inconclusive report remains valuable (e.g., identifies a decisive next measurement) and when it would **not** satisfy a buyer. Do not manufacture ROI or set a savings-linked price before evidence.

**Exit evidence:** Reviewer route, feasible delivery method, and explicit commercial unknowns. No price or timeline claim without a real basis.

#### Two distinct financial views (never confuse them)

- **Cash case contribution:** collected fee minus *cash case-specific costs actually incurred* (reviewer invoice, travel, paid processing, external help, etc.). If the fee is merely quoted or invoiced, no collection has occurred.
- **Economically adjusted case contribution:** collected fee minus the same cash direct costs **and non-duplicated founder/analyst labor at a disclosed shadow rate**. This is a decision-useful sustainability proxy, not audited company profit.
- **Break-even fee estimate:** expected cash direct costs + analyst/founder labor value + risk/rework allowance. Separately consider acquisition burden and shared overhead before deciding a business is attractive. Do **not** add founder time twice.
- **Capacity constraint:** log scope creep, evidence clean-up, adviser wait time, on-site travel, report rework and payment collection. These may dominate software runtime. If a case requires unlimited expert hours or unbounded site attendance, *do not sell it on fixed-price terms*.

**Pricing experiment, not a price announcement:** ask a real budget owner to react to a defined scope with a concrete proposed price and terms. Record **accepted / counteroffered / declined / not reached**, reasons and competing spend. ₹15,000 remains an unvalidated placeholder, not a recommendation. Do not invent a target gross margin; pre-register a minimum acceptable economics threshold once real delivery costs and available cash are known.

#### Qualified-review operating contract (required before Tier 2)

| Required item | Specific decision to record |
|---|---|
| Reviewer competence | C1 maintenance/reliability **or** C4 electrical/tariff expertise relevant to the exact case |
| Scope of review | Which sources, calculations, hypotheses and technical statements the reviewer is responsible for |
| Deliverable & record | Written feedback, dated approval/rejection, revisions, unresolved dissent and report version |
| Availability & terms | Expected turnaround, hours or fixed fee, maximum work, rework policy and escalation |
| Independence & conflicts | Disclose supplier/vendor incentives or commercial conflicts affecting objectivity |
| Liability and safety boundary | No independent operation of plant assets, electrical testing or safety-critical instruction by GridMind |
| Fallback | If unavailable or reviewer cannot establish conclusions, **pause the technical engagement**; do not replace review with AI text |

**Owner/reviewer responsibility split:** GridMind coordinates, documents and analyzes within competence; the reviewer judges scoped technical conclusions; the factory authorizes access and owns physical interventions and operational decisions. Neither a friendly expert chat nor an unsigned proposal counts as contracted qualified review.

**Required tests:** known-answer calculations, missing/duplicate/invalid/contradictory evidence, uncertain tariff interpretation, wrong time units, reviewer rejection, report gating, retained corrections, role boundaries, cross-tenant access denial, private-file access denial, and reload/recovery. Where persistence changes, run relevant migration/security/Edge Function/browser acceptance on an isolated environment.

**Required operations:** qualified reviewer available and contracted for the case; factory-side decision authority; written data permission and retention policy; approved contact/visit procedure; escalation of uncertain findings; reviewer and delivery time budget.

**Exit evidence:** Named accountable roles, tests/results attached to the correct revision, tested information boundaries and a clear STOP route. A passing CI run is not itself a pilot authorization.

### Stage 7 — One controlled, authorized factory pilot

1. Pre-register the customer's problem, available evidence, best current alternative, expected decision, comparison metrics and exclusions **before** inspecting results.
2. Obtain written authorization, permissions and required qualified involvement; minimize data collection.
3. Execute the investigation; record every human and automated step, time spent and revisions.
4. Obtain substantive expert review; issue the approved brief or a truthful inconclusive output.
5. Ask the decision owner: **What decision can you now make that your old process could not support?** Record action and objections.
6. Where a safe relevant follow-up exists, measure the actual result and uncertainty. Keep billing arithmetic, possible exposure and realized benefits distinct.
7. Complete case economics; compare usefulness, effort, time and total cost with the current alternative.

**Exit evidence:** One real buyer-evaluated brief, documented reviewer acceptance/limitations, actual case costs and buyer feedback; not just a demo video.

### Stage 7.5 — First-paying-customer experiment (a separate commercial test)

**Purpose:** Measure whether a real budget holder will purchase the *defined decision*, rather than praise a report or software demo. Only conduct it after the applicable buyer, professional-support, safety and data-rights gates.

1. **Identify buyer and problem:** decision owner, budget approver, urgency, current method, and the specific unresolved gap. Avoid contacting factories without an appropriate legitimate introduction/support route.
2. **Offer a bounded option:** one-page SOW specifying exact question and scope, allowed inputs, responsible reviewer, deliverables, assumptions/exclusions, acceptance and inconclusive/stop rules.
3. **Present a price hypothesis transparently:** record quote, assumptions and customer counteroffer/objections. Do not make ROI, accuracy or savings guarantees.
4. **Request a real buying action:** proposal revision with budget owner, signed pilot/paid scope, or order approval. A positive interview is **not** a purchase.
5. **Separate outcomes in the ledger:** proposed → buyer-reviewed → approved/contracted → delivered/accepted → invoiced → collected. Timestamp actual events; never backfill imaginary demand.
6. **Evaluate the decision:** buyer value **and** actual service economics **and** technical quality must be acceptable. If buyer declines, record whether the cause was no pain, strong incumbent, price, low trust, data access or timing.

**Early success:** a paid engagement is agreed and delivered with accurate cost records and an accepted, reviewed brief. **Stronger success:** cash is collected and a distinct second customer takes a comparable step. **Do not call this product-market fit.**

### Stage 8 — Commercial validation, then automation

- Propose a bounded paid engagement with actual price, scope, exclusions, reviewer model and agreed acceptance conditions.
- Track **quote → signed agreement → delivery → invoice → collection** separately. Do not count verbal interest as revenue.
- Repeat with independent customers, analyze where labor/rework accumulates, and automate the *measured bottleneck* that lowers effort or errors without undermining review.
- Add subscriptions, recurring monitoring, a secure customer portal, advanced dashboards or sensor integrations **only when customer behavior and economics justify them**.

## 6. Minimum information architecture and trust model

Follow the **eight existing canonical objects** rather than creating eight new database tables without justification:

1. **Investigation:** organization boundary; decision question; scope/time/site; owner; lifecycle; stop conditions.
2. **Evidence:** origin/owner; authorization; original reference; timestamps; quality; rights; retention; permitted audience.
3. **Timeline event or period record:** raw value/time and normalized interpretation, reconciliation, links to sources.
4. **Finding/claim/hypothesis:** assertion, evidence for and against, alternatives, uncertainty, next discriminatory check.
5. **Calculation:** input IDs; formula/version; units; assumptions; excluded records; output and sensitivity.
6. **Review:** reviewer role/competence; scope; challenge; revisions; acceptance/rejection; unresolved dissent.
7. **Customer decision:** buyer's action/inaction, justification, owner, date and follow-up requirements.
8. **Outcome check:** metric; baseline; observation window; confounders; source; status from planned to verified.

**Commercial operations metadata** (SOW, hours, costs, payment status) should be added minimally and separately from technical evidence, with correct access controls. Do not expose private prices or unrelated customer information across tenants.

### Non-negotiable invariants

- Real factory records never enter unapproved synthetic workflows or third-party AI systems.
- All customer access is scoped, authorized and tested; organization A cannot read organization B's files or rows.
- Original sources, transformations, corrections and report versions stay traceable under agreed custody/retention rules.
- Missing or invalid evidence is never silently replaced with zero or model guesses.
- Observation ≠ correlation ≠ causal explanation ≠ intervention recommendation ≠ realized saving.
- Deterministic arithmetic is reproducible; AI assistance is labeled and checked by humans.
- No material technical claim receives implied reviewer approval; competent staff retain control of physical work and safety-sensitive decisions.
- A technically correct but *commercially unhelpful* investigation is not counted as product validation.

| Repeat willingness/purchase | Buyer-requested follow-up or second paid scope | Early repeatability signal; one repeat not a moat |

**Core economics:** `Collected fee − case-specific direct delivery costs = case contribution` (not necessarily profit). Track founder time, acquisition cost and overhead in separate, non-duplicative fields. Any ₹15,000 example remains hypothetical until the quote, reviewer and buyer validate it.

### Pilot comparison protocol: do not grade ourselves against an imaginary weak baseline

Before examining any real case data, record the customer's current alternative and the decision it can/cannot answer. Compare *equivalent scope*, not a 90-minute GridMind screening against a comprehensive engineer-led audit. Track results separately:

| Dimension | Measurement | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Time to decision | Elapsed hours/days from equivalent starting condition to a reviewed decision | Time stamps + baseline estimates labeled as estimates |
| Buyer/staff effort | Actual hours spent by plant staff, GridMind analyst and reviewer | Effort logs; account for extra data-cleanup requests |
| Total cost of the decision | Factory internal labor + payments + travel + any implementation required to reach same decision | Disclosed assumptions and actual receipts/time |
| Quality and traceability | Material claims with source link, error/correction count, reviewer disputes and unresolved limitations | Report audit and independent expert critique |
| Decision usefulness | Concrete change in decision, requested check or evidence gap resolved | Buyer statement and action record, not Likert satisfaction alone |
| Safety/trust | Unauthorized access attempts denied, no unsupported safety intervention or claim | Security-test evidence + reviewer release record |

**Decision rule:** We do *not* need to outperform the incumbent on every metric. We do need a buyer-meaningful advantage on at least one dimension **without material deterioration in safety, technical quality or overall economics**. If the same result can be delivered better by the factory's own expert, narrow or abandon that offer instead of advertising a fictitious advantage.

### Differentiation hypothesis to prove

> GridMind makes a difficult industrial decision easier to reach through bounded on-site investigation, source-linked evidence and qualified review—**without requiring the customer to adopt a complete industrial management system**.

Test this against local engineers, existing records, consultants, auditors, and appropriate maintenance/energy platforms. Avoid claims of being universally “cheaper” or “more accurate” until actual comparative data supports them.

## 12. Unresolved decisions (must be answered by evidence, not preference)

1. **Who buys?** Owner, plant head, operations lead, energy manager or finance? Identify payer versus technical reviewer/user.
2. **Which first problem?** C1 or C4, based on independent buyer evidence and unresolved alternative gap.
3. **Why us?** What unique value can we deliver beyond the buyer's current engineer/vendor/consultant?
4. **What evidence can be legally and practically obtained?** What remains unavailable?
5. **Who is qualified to review?** Competence, terms, liability boundary, availability and fee.
6. **What is the viable cost/price relationship?** Actual hours, reviewer expense and payment behavior.
7. **When is software necessary?** Which measured manual step is expensive or error-prone enough to justify automation?

**Default when evidence is insufficient:** Continue bounded discovery, a synthetic method test or narrowly scoped correction—not a broad product build.
