## Executive decision

Advance **Clarity Engineered** as the leading creative direction for GridMind's visual exploration. It means making the hidden logic of industrial complexity visible through precise, evidence-aware storytelling.

This is approval to explore a creative direction—not approval of a final visual design, headline, palette, first commercial wedge, product claim, or production implementation. The complete direction, principles, constraints, prototype criteria, and acceptance criteria are recorded in [GridMind Creative Direction — Clarity Engineered](gridmind-creative-direction-clarity-engineered-2026-10-11.md).

The design ambition is Apple-level coherence, Nothing-level recognisability, and ambitious interactive storytelling, translated into an original GridMind identity rather than copied brand styling.

## Current creative direction

**Working concept:** Clarity Engineered  
**Core principle:** Make the complexity visible; make the evidence legible; make the next decision understandable.

### Motion grammar
1. **Reveal** — bring meaningful context into view.
2. **Relate** — align relevant evidence on a shared frame, scale, or timeline.
3. **Investigate** — focus on a specific question without losing orientation.
4. **Resolve** — make observations, hypotheses, missing evidence, and a possible next decision legible.

These are semantic principles, not a compulsory sequence of effects. No decorative motion, generic glowing networks, fake telemetry, unsupported causal links, cursor ripples, rejected neon-lime topographic hero, or glowing Data → Detect → Quantify → Verify rail.

## Execution sequence

### Step 1 — Keep the commercial question active
Continue the existing buyer/problem discovery and compare the three candidate wedges in the Decision Constitution: operational-loss/constraint investigation, procurement intelligence for one bounded category, and one narrow vertical operations workflow. Do not choose energy simply because it is easy to visualize or already documented.

The visual prototype may use a synthetic scenario, but its purpose is to test communication—not to validate the market or claim a product capability.

### Step 2 — Produce three comparable visual explorations
Use the same Clarity Engineered design philosophy, but test three different visual entry points:

1. **Context Arrives:** a stable measurement becomes more interpretable as production context and operating events align around it.
2. **The Operation in Layers:** a simplified process composition reveals the relevant layer for a selected operational question.
3. **Evidence Changes the Decision:** a possible action is unpacked into observations, assumptions, missing evidence, and trade-offs.

Each must include:
- a strong static opening frame;
- a three-frame motion storyboard;
- one meaningful visitor interaction;
- clear labels and plain-language explanation;
- a closing state and provisional next action;
- mobile and reduced-motion states;
- an explicit claim/capability boundary.

Do not build three unrelated brands or three versions of a generic diagram. Each exploration should express one coherent GridMind identity.

### Step 3 — Compare with a fixed rubric
Score each from 1–5. These are internal judgments unless buyer-tested.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Brand recognisability and originality | 25% |
| Immediate comprehension | 20% |
| Motion meaning and craft | 20% |
| Industrial credibility and evidence integrity | 15% |
| Commercial relevance | 10% |
| Accessibility and performance feasibility | 10% |

For each score, record why, what remains uncertain, and what observation would change the score. Do not let “looks cool” or the highest total alone determine the winner; a serious credibility or comprehension failure can disqualify a concept.

### Step 4 — Test comprehension, not just preference
Where feasible, show the explorations in randomized order to relevant industrial decision-makers without explaining the intended message first. Ask:
- What do you think GridMind does?
- What does the visual appear to promise?
- Which elements seem credible or suspicious?
- What remains unclear?
- What business question would you bring to GridMind?
- What next step would you take?

Capture confusion, skepticism, and mistaken assumptions. If direct buyer access is not available, label the review internal and keep the direction provisional.

### Step 5 — Select or revise
Choose a direction only if it combines distinctiveness with fast comprehension, evidence integrity, and credible business relevance. If none passes, revise the concepts rather than forcing a winner.

### Step 6 — Build one bounded prototype
After choosing a concept for further exploration, build only the hero-to-investigation sequence as an isolated prototype. Preserve the existing product route. Use deterministic synthetic data with a visible label. Test desktop, mobile, keyboard/touch, reduced motion, readability, loading performance, and whether motion adds understanding.

### Step 7 — Keep product and commercial gates intact
Before full public-site implementation:
- refresh the product-truth/readiness audit;
- define the honest first offer and exclusions;
- map public claims to demonstrated capabilities and evidence;
- confirm CTA and form behavior, privacy/data handling, security language, accessibility, and performance;
- keep synthetic marketing scenarios separate from product proof.

## Guardrails

- Ambition broad; immediate commitment narrow.
- Visual excellence is a priority, not a substitute for evidence or commercial validation.
- No invented customer proof, savings, integrations, security guarantees, product capabilities, or results.
- Clearly label synthetic data and hypothetical findings.
- Do not imply causation from visual proximity or correlation alone.
- Do not add motion that blocks comprehension or the next action.
- Preserve the existing product route and architecture.
- Update the decision record when evidence changes a decision; keep earlier choices traceable through GitHub history.

## Plan improvements — execution control and first-principles validation (2026-10-11)

This section tightens the plan from a creative brief into an execution system. It does not override the GridMind Decision Constitution; where there is a conflict, the Constitution takes precedence.

### Improvement 1 — Run three workstreams in parallel, with explicit dependencies

**A. Commercial discovery (start now; do not wait for design):**
- Compare the three candidate wedges: operational-loss/constraint investigation; procurement intelligence for one bounded category; and a narrow vertical operations workflow.
- Score each candidate 1–5 against the following weighted criteria: problem severity/frequency (20%); economic consequence (20%); willingness-to-pay evidence (20%); access to customers and relevant data (15%); credible first-delivery feasibility (15%); expansion potential (10%).
- For every score, record the evidence, confidence level, critical assumption, and what observation would change the score. Unknowns are not zeroes and should not be disguised as facts.
- Conduct problem interviews with relevant industrial decision-makers when access is available. Ask about the last real occurrence, existing workaround, time/cost consequences, data availability, current alternatives, budget ownership, procurement friction, and evidence needed to trust a solution.
- Do not call a candidate validated based only on compliments, hypothetical interest, or a polished prototype. Record interview evidence and willingness-to-pay signals separately.

**B. Product truth and delivery feasibility:**
- Maintain a capability register with four statuses: demonstrated today; implemented but not yet verified; proposed; explicitly out of scope.
- For each potential public claim, record the exact wording, supporting evidence, capability status, limitation, and approval decision.
- Document required inputs, outputs, human review, failure modes, data permissions, security/privacy implications, and manual work needed for the first offer.
- Do not imply integrations, real-time monitoring, autonomous diagnosis, causal attribution, verified savings, security certifications, or customer proof unless demonstrated and appropriately evidenced.

**C. Brand and experience:**
- Build the isolated The Cut prototype and test whether the interaction improves comprehension, not just whether it looks polished.
- Keep the creative direction provisional until the interaction and message are tested. Do not let prototype completion select the commercial wedge by default.

### Improvement 2 — Use a transparent opportunity scorecard

For each candidate, include:
1. Problem and named buyer/user.
2. Triggering event and frequency.
3. Current workaround and alternatives.
4. Cost of the status quo, including how the estimate was obtained.
5. Evidence of urgency and willingness to pay.
6. Data access, quality, permission, and integration burden.
7. A smallest credible deliverable and its human-in-the-loop boundaries.
8. Time and resources needed to deliver the first outcome.
9. Competitive/substitute options, including spreadsheets, consultants, incumbent software, and doing nothing.
10. Expansion path if the first use case works.

A weighted score is a prioritization aid, not a substitute for judgment. Include confidence and downside risks next to the score. If a candidate scores well because several assumptions are unknown, run tests against those assumptions before committing.

### Improvement 3 — Define measurable prototype acceptance criteria before polishing

The Cut prototype is ready for review only when all of these are true:
- The initial static frame communicates a coherent industrial investigation without requiring animation.
- The visitor can change context using an explicit control; the selected state changes annotations and evidence, not only colour.
- The underlying illustrative measurement remains stable while the contextual layer changes, and the experience does not imply causation from proximity alone.
- The evidence panel separates observations within the synthetic scenario, possible explanations that are not established, and missing evidence/next checks.
- The synthetic-data disclaimer remains visible and understandable on desktop and mobile.
- All essential content remains available with reduced motion and without JavaScript-driven animation.
- Core controls are keyboard accessible, visibly focused, labelled for assistive technology, and usable by touch.
- At narrow widths, the evidence and controls remain readable without horizontal overflow.
- The interaction does not block the core message, hijack scrolling, simulate live telemetry, or require a third-party service.
- The prototype stays outside the production route and does not alter `src/routes/index.tsx`.

These are acceptance conditions, not a claim that the prototype has already passed them. Record the actual verification method and results. If a browser/device check was not performed, mark it not tested.

### Improvement 4 — Test understanding, recall, and false inferences

Run a lightweight, documented test with people unfamiliar with GridMind. Where possible, include target industrial buyers; label internal-only feedback as internal and provisional.

**Before explaining the prototype, ask:**
- What do you think this is for?
- What changed when you used the context control?
- What do you think GridMind can do today?
- What evidence is shown, and what remains unknown?
- What would you inspect or ask next?

After a short unrelated interval, ask what they remember. Track:
- correct explanation of intended purpose;
- correct understanding of the interaction;
- mistaken assumptions about live data, integrations, automation, causation, or verified outcomes;
- recall of the distinctive signature;
- accessibility or usability failures;
- perceived relevance to a real operational problem.

Record participant type, date, task, observed response, issue severity, and resulting change. Do not treat a tiny convenience sample as statistically representative. If the prototype causes repeated false capability assumptions, revise the design and labels before adding polish.

### Improvement 5 — Validate the offer separately from the design

Before approving the production homepage, write a one-page offer brief containing:
- initial customer profile and buyer;
- specific triggering problem and current alternative;
- deliverable and explicit exclusions;
- required customer inputs/data and permissions;
- delivery method and human involvement;
- what success would mean and how it would be measured;
- likely objections, purchasing path, and CTA;
- evidence still missing.

A credible first offer may be narrow and partly manual if that is what can be delivered honestly. Do not disguise a proposed service as an existing software capability. Do not publish price, savings, or outcome guarantees without a defensible basis.

### Improvement 6 — Add decision gates with stop/revise rules

| Gate | Required evidence | Pass decision | Stop/revise condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1: Initial problem | Comparative opportunity scorecard; key assumptions and evidence gaps; initial customer/problem evidence where access permits | Select one provisional wedge for testing | No candidate has a credible problem, reachable buyer, or feasible data path |
| G2: Product truth | Capability register, delivery boundaries, risk/failure-mode review | Approve only claims that match demonstrated evidence | The proposed promise depends on unbuilt or inaccessible capabilities |
| G3: Interactive prototype | Acceptance checks, documented technical review, comprehension/false-inference findings | Refine and carry forward a defensible experience | Visitors misunderstand the core idea or infer unsupported capabilities |
| G4: Offer | Clear buyer, problem, deliverable, inputs, exclusions, measurement, and credible customer-interest evidence | Approve a provisional offer and CTA | The problem is not urgent enough, delivery is not credible, or evidence is too weak |
| G5: Production website | Approved offer and claims; privacy/security/accessibility/performance review; CTA and data-handling design | Authorize bounded production implementation | Any critical truth, security, privacy, or usability issue remains unresolved |

Passing a gate means the listed evidence exists and has been reviewed; it does not mean every commercial uncertainty disappears.

### Improvement 7 — Instrument the future site around meaningful outcomes

Before launch, define what each conversion event means. Candidate events include qualified inquiry, completed assessment request, booked discovery conversation, or another action that matches the actual offer. Choose only after the offer is validated.

For every event, document:
- event name and precise definition;
- user action and required properties;
- analytics/privacy implications and consent requirements;
- baseline availability and reporting owner;
- what decision the metric will inform.

Do not optimize for raw clicks or animation engagement if they do not predict qualified demand. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal or factory-sensitive data.

### Improvement 8 — Make the project record operational

At the end of every substantive work session, update the relevant GitHub record with:
- decision/change and rationale;
- evidence and confidence;
- assumptions and unresolved questions;
- files/code changed and commit link;
- tests actually run and results;
- failures or rejected alternatives;
- next action and its completion condition;
- conditions that would trigger reconsideration.

Keep decisions traceable rather than overwriting uncertainty with certainty. Distinguish **planned**, **implemented**, **tested**, and **validated**. A file or code commit proves implementation of an artifact, not customer demand or product effectiveness.

## Priority reset — commercial viability before further visual polish (2026-10-11)

### Why the priority changed

A factory saving only ₹2,000–₹3,000 per month may not support a viable GridMind offer. The question is not simply whether GridMind can produce some savings; it is whether a specific buyer receives enough credible incremental value to justify the fee, adoption burden, risk, and change-management effort—and whether GridMind can deliver that outcome at sustainable cost.

This is now a **critical commercial thesis risk**. A real problem is not automatically a good business. Do not defend the concept by inflating savings, inventing a higher-value use case, or treating a prototype as proof. If the economics are weak, narrow or abandon the candidate and test another one.

The scorecard now includes a commercial economics gate: [GridMind Initial Commercial Wedge Scorecard](gridmind-initial-commercial-wedge-scorecard-2026-10-11.md#commercial-economics-gate--required-before-choosing-a-wedge).

### Revised next milestone

**Milestone: produce an evidence-backed commercial viability decision for one narrowly defined industrial problem.** The deliverable is not a prettier homepage. It is a decision record that shows the problem, buyer, status quo, conservative incremental value, purchase path, data access, delivery process, and plausible GridMind unit economics—or clearly concludes that current evidence is insufficient.

### Execution order

1. **Define reachable buyer segments.** List specific people we can realistically approach, their roles, likely operating context, and how contact is possible. Do not claim access until confirmed.
2. **Run problem-first discovery.** Ask for the last real occurrence, frequency, current workaround, operational and financial consequence, existing alternatives, decision owner, and what remains unresolved. Do not lead with the GridMind concept or ask only whether someone likes AI.
3. **Quantify economics conservatively.** Record low/base/high estimates, sources, attribution, recurrence, confidence, buyer hurdle, adoption costs, and a path to measure the outcome. Treat a few thousand rupees of monthly benefit as a warning when the delivery model is high-touch.
4. **Map current alternatives.** Include existing staff/processes, spreadsheets, incumbent industrial software, vendors/consultants, and doing nothing. GridMind must beat the buyer's real alternative—not an imaginary empty market.
5. **Check data and delivery feasibility.** Identify the minimum records needed, who owns them, permission constraints, data quality, human/domain review, failure modes, and the smallest useful output that can be delivered honestly.
6. **Model GridMind unit economics.** Estimate qualification/sales, onboarding, expert time, support, hosting/inference, integrations, QA/rework, contract value, gross margin, and founder hours. Mark estimates as estimates until observed.
7. **Test willingness to pay behaviorally.** Seek a specific next step with a credible buyer: a scoped proposal discussion, budget-owner introduction, permitted sample-data review, or paid-pilot discussion. Compliments and hypothetical interest do not validate demand.
8. **Compare candidates A/B/C only after evidence collection.** Use the existing weighted scorecard, attach confidence and source to each score, and keep unknowns visible. Select a provisional wedge only if it clears the economics and feasibility gates.
9. **Then shape the first offer and site narrative.** Explain the verified pain, specific deliverable, required inputs, exclusions, measurement method, and CTA. Keep all claims within demonstrated product truth.
10. **Resume visual iteration only when it answers a real commercial communication need.** The existing prototype remains a communication experiment, not a market signal. Browser/device verification is still outstanding and must be recorded honestly.

### Decision thresholds without fake precision

Do not impose one universal rupee threshold across all factories or business models. Establish the threshold with the actual buyer and economics:
- Is the conservative incremental value material relative to fees and adoption cost?
- Does the outcome clear that buyer's required return, risk reduction, or payback?
- Is the problem frequent enough for recurring revenue, or should it be a paid one-off investigation?
- Can GridMind retain adequate gross profit after real delivery/support costs?
- Does the solution remain valuable after existing tools and normal process improvements are accounted for?

If these cannot be answered, the decision is **not yet validated**—not “yes” by optimism and not “no” solely because one early estimate is small.

### Exit criteria for this milestone

The milestone is complete only when the project record contains:
- at least one real, recent problem narrative from a relevant buyer, or an explicit record that buyer access is still a blocker;
- a baseline/workaround and comparable alternatives;
- a transparent conservative value estimate with uncertainty and attribution;
- the adoption burden, budget owner, buying process, and actual willingness-to-pay signal (or a clear absence of one);
- data permissions and a credible minimum delivery method;
- a first-pass GridMind cost-to-serve and gross-margin model;
- an evidence-backed decision to proceed, narrow, test further, or reject the candidate.

No such buyer evidence is being claimed by this plan update. The immediate work is to collect it.
