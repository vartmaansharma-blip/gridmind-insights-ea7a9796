# GridMind Decision Constitution and Contradiction Register
**Decision date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Canonical interpretation of the current strategy. This file resolves conflicts between planning documents; it does not claim customer validation or product readiness.

## Why this exists
GridMind's strategy is recorded across business-thesis, expansion, website, use-case, improvement-priority and execution documents. Some describe future possibilities, provisional candidates, or specifications that can be mistaken for current commitments. This register establishes one source of truth and an explicit decision hierarchy.

## 1. Decision hierarchy
If documents appear to disagree, use this order:

1. **Safety, authorization, data rights and security constraints** — always override roadmap or commercial urgency.
2. **This Decision Constitution** — resolves current strategic interpretation and scope.
3. **Newest explicit decision record supported by evidence** — supersedes older decisions only when it names the change and evidence.
4. **Current execution plan** — defines order of work, not proof that work has passed.
5. **Product specifications and data contracts** — implementation guidance only inside approved scope.
6. **Long-term thesis, expansion maps, ratings, marketing concepts and visual explorations** — hypotheses or destination, not immediate build commitments.

A new idea does not supersede a prior decision just because it is newer. To change a decision, record the previous decision, proposed change, evidence, trade-offs, risks, date, and affected documents.

## 2. Canonical decisions

### D1 — Long-term company thesis
**Decision:** GridMind's long-term ambition is one industrial intelligence and value-realization platform: contextualize operations, qualify evidence, investigate, prioritize decisions, coordinate approved action and verify outcomes.

**Interpretation:** This is the destination, not a promise that every capability exists. Build the shared core incrementally where real cases prove reuse. Do not treat it as a mandate to implement a universal factory ontology or every industrial vertical now.

### D2 — Immediate scope
**Decision:** For this 30-day cycle, establish current product truth and validate one narrow customer problem—not expand the platform or polish the marketing website.

**In scope:** current-state audit; authorized live-workflow verification; customer/problem discovery; candidate comparison; data feasibility; bounded offer; minimum technical fixes needed for the selected case; fair evaluation and Day-30 decision.

**Out of scope unless evidence changes the decision:** marketplace, autonomous industrial control, broad integrations, multi-site benchmarking, generalized ontology expansion, multiple vertical modules, speculative data-centre offering, and full public-site implementation before offer/claim validation.

### D3 — First commercial wedge is NOT decided
**Decision:** No permanent first wedge has been selected. The current comparison set is five problem families:
1. Downtime / production loss.
2. Quality defects / scrap / rework.
3. Maintenance recurrence / reliability.
4. Energy / material inefficiency.
5. Cross-functional decision / handoff delay.

Procurement intelligence remains a possible adjacent or alternative opportunity only if discovery narrows it to a specific purchasing category, buyer, recurring decision, and measurable consequence. Energy intensity for a bounded asset/process has an existing candidate data contract; detail does not confer priority.

**Current discovery probes (not wedge choices):** probe C1 (downtime/production-loss investigation) and C4 (energy/material inefficiency) deeply, to avoid spreading ten conversations across five families. Reopen another stable candidate only if evidence materially weakens both probes or a buyer introduces a stronger specific case. A retrospective test is a later stage, only after Tier 1 evidence and Tier 2 permissions/reviewer/baseline gates.

**Selection evidence:** severity/frequency; conservative addressable economics; buyer and budget ownership; current alternatives; authorized data access; credible baseline; qualified reviewer; time-to-useful-result; influenceability; willingness to pay; delivery burden and margin; safety/trust; repeatability; and expansion potential. Fatal blockers override scores. If evidence is insufficient, continue discovery rather than force a winner.

### D4 — Product readiness and claims
**Decision:** Repository files, a functioning-looking UI, a green deployment, synthetic examples and completed documentation do not independently establish production readiness or customer value.

Before describing a flow as working, record the actual build/commit/environment, test steps, observed outcomes and failures. Before pilot-ready, verify the authorized end-to-end path and required security/persistence checks. Before accuracy or superiority claims, use a pre-registered comparison and qualified review. Before savings claims, measure outcomes after an approved intervention.

### D5 — Marketing website
**Decision:** Homepage and marketing/sales strategy documents are design and messaging hypotheses, not authorization to implement the full public website now and not evidence of product capabilities.

When the website is built, its goal is qualified problem-fit conversations, not unqualified demo bookings or guaranteed savings. “Hidden operational losses” is a hero hypothesis to test against evidence-led alternatives, not a settled winner. Public claims must match capabilities actually demonstrated. Use clearly labelled illustrative/synthetic examples; never invent customer logos, telemetry, results or case studies.

**Gate before full implementation:** refresh product truth; define the honest first offer and exclusions; test positioning with relevant buyers; map every public capability/result claim to evidence and readiness. Small research or copy tests are allowed if they do not distract from these gates.

### D6 — Build strategy
**Decision:** Build the smallest reliable vertical slice needed to test the selected workflow. Reuse the shared core only where it reduces delivery cost or improves decision quality in evidence. Do not build features solely because they appear on the long-term platform map.

Expected workflow, where relevant:
authorized data → input validation/context → evidence-backed analysis → qualified review → customer-approved decision/action → outcome measurement.

The exact workflow depends on the selected use case. Not every candidate needs an energy engine, all factory entities, or the same integrations.

### D7 — Human authority and evidence
**Decision:** GridMind supports industrial professionals; it does not autonomously authorize safety-critical, production-critical or material purchasing decisions. Findings must separate observation, inference, hypothesis, recommendation, approved action, measured result and verified outcome. Insufficient evidence must produce a clear refusal or request for discriminating evidence.

### D8 — Day-30 outcome
**Decision:** Day 30 produces an evidence-backed GO / NARROW / PIVOT / STOP decision. A paid pilot is a target, not a guaranteed result. If access, data rights, review or buyer evidence is missing, record the blocker honestly and plan the next test. No fabricated factory data or fake validation.

### D9 — Concept and product-expansion principles

**Decision:** GridMind is a proposed industrial decision-and-value-realization layer. Its central hypothesis is that connecting authorized operational evidence to investigation, human-approved action, and verified outcomes can improve some consequential industrial workflows beyond existing alternatives.

**Binding principles:**
1. **Value before features:** every capability must serve a validated customer need or a clearly stated, testable assumption.
2. **Evidence before claims:** label facts, estimates, hypotheses, expert judgments, and verified outcomes separately.
3. **Narrow entry point, expandable architecture:** keep the long-term platform as a destination; do not build multiple workflows before the first is demonstrated.
4. **Existing alternatives are the baseline:** compare GridMind against current software, expert review, integrations, and simpler process changes.
5. **Incremental value must be demonstrated:** better analysis is not automatically a better decision, and a better decision is not automatically realized savings.
6. **Expansion must earn approval:** require repeatability, buyer evidence, security and data-rights compliance, measurable outcomes, and a credible delivery model.

**Unknown, not decided:** the initial industry segment, economic buyer, first workflow, pricing, willingness to pay, delivery model, and durable competitive advantage. The five opportunity territories in the bigger-problem thesis are candidates, not validated rankings. No customer demand, savings, or technical readiness is implied.

**Change rule:** revise these principles only through an explicit decision record containing new evidence, trade-offs, risks, date, and affected documents.

**Long-term thesis:** GridMind aims to become a trusted industrial decision-and-improvement layer that helps teams identify costly operational losses or decision bottlenecks, establish evidence, coordinate human-approved interventions, and verify outcomes. This is a platform ambition, not a current capability claim.

The first wedge remains undecided. Compare: (1) industrial loss detection and verified recovery; (2) cross-system operational decision intelligence; (3) recurrence prevention/corrective-action effectiveness; (4) quality escapes and supplier-to-customer traceability; and (5) energy/material/productivity optimization. ## Canonical conceptual clarification — 2026-10-11

**Previous state:** GridMind was described broadly as an industrial intelligence and value-realization platform. That remains the long-term destination, but by itself it is too abstract to govern the first product.

**Clarification:** GridMind's product contract is to help one industrial decision owner make one consequential operating decision better by turning authorized, imperfect evidence into a traceable finding, a reviewed next action, and a measurable outcome. Platform breadth is earned only by repeating this loop across validated workflows.

### Binding consequences
1. A dashboard, chatbot, anomaly, report, or plausible AI explanation is not by itself customer value.
2. Every proposed first use case must name a user, economic buyer, triggering event, decision, current workaround, observable failure, required authorized data, bounded deliverable, qualified reviewer, outcome metric, and reason to choose GridMind over alternatives.
3. The system must distinguish source observations, calculations, hypotheses, causal claims, recommendations, approved interventions, estimated opportunity, and verified outcomes.
4. If the evidence is insufficient, contradictory, stale, incomparable, or unauthorized, GridMind must disclose the gap or stop; it must not manufacture certainty.
5. Consequential operational action remains under the customer's qualified human review, authorization, and safety/change-control process. No autonomous industrial control is implied.
6. Economic outputs require traceable inputs, explicit assumptions, versioned deterministic calculations, and uncertainty. Estimated opportunity is not realized savings.
7. Do not build universal ontology, broad integrations, multi-vertical modules, or a new marketing surface merely to make the concept feel complete. Build only the smallest workflow justified by evidence.
8. Do not pick a first wedge because a specification already exists or implementation looks easiest. Compare candidates against the same buyer, data, outcome, and delivery gates.
9. If an existing tool, expert, or simpler process is sufficient, state that plainly; GridMind must demonstrate incremental value rather than force AI into the workflow.
10. A reusable component or moat is only a hypothesis until multiple permissioned cases demonstrate repeatability and customers accept the value.

**Immediate execution effect:** The 30-day goal remains to select, narrow, or reject the first commercial workflow based on real evidence. Product definition is now more precise; product-market fit, customer demand, live workflow correctness, and commercial readiness remain unproven.

## Concept refinement — decision-to-verified-outcome contract (2026-10-11)

The product definition now sharpens the broad industrial-intelligence thesis into a decision-level contract.

**Binding interpretation:** GridMind's initial product is a decision-to-verified-outcome workflow for one consequential industrial decision. Its value is not an alert or generated report; it is a traceable finding that can inform a human decision and, where action is approved, support measurement of the result.

Keep these lifecycle states distinct:
1. Signal — something may warrant attention.
2. Finding — a claim supported by identified evidence and limitations.
3. Opportunity — a possible improvement with estimated, not realized, value.
4. Decision/action — a choice approved by the responsible human owner.
5. Outcome — a measured result against a predeclared baseline and window, with attribution limits.

Before a candidate is scored, apply hard gates for: named buyer/owner; concrete recent event; addressable decision; permissioned usable data; credible baseline and outcome metric; plausible incremental advantage over existing alternatives; safe and bounded delivery; and a concrete commercial next step. Weighted scores must not hide fatal blockers or unknowns.

The first offer may be manual-first and software-assisted to test whether a real result is valuable. Automate repeated steps only after observing them. This does not establish that GridMind should become a consulting agency; it is a way to avoid building a generalized platform before knowing what repeats.

The product must be willing to report “insufficient evidence,” “existing process is adequate,” “not controllable,” “not economically justified,” and “no measurable improvement.” These are valid outcomes, not product failures to conceal.

**Unchanged:** first commercial wedge remains undecided. The next action is evidence-led buyer and workflow discovery, not broad feature expansion.

## Execution amendment — 2026-10-11 (binding)

Track connector agreement, introduction made, recipient reply, booking, and held call separately. The 10% end-to-end rate is an untested rough estimate. Advisor review is not a universal gate for low-risk, non-confidential public research outreach; it is required before making technical judgements that need domain expertise. Customer data, site access, OT access, contracts, and payments require appropriate authorization and applicable safety/security/eligibility checks.
- **Safety boundary:** no OT/SCADA/PLC/MES connection, control commands, writes, or remote access during discovery or the first pilot. Use a customer-approved controlled export; any later read-only path requires explicit authorization, least privilege, logging, and qualified site/security review.

On Nov 10, if no candidate meets the evidence bar, no-go on new GridMind build; choose a bounded narrow test, pivot to the ranked narrow B2B AI service-to-software/productized-service alternative, or stop. Confirm actual time, runway, and near-term income requirement before committing another sprint.
